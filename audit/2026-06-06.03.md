## 0 · About this review

This is a **maximum-rigor internal cryptographic review** of the zkCoins specification, performed by an AI reviewer. It is not, and does not claim to be, an external accredited audit. It is the most thorough analysis I can produce, with explicit confidence labels per claim so the reader can make their own risk decision.

**What I bring.**

- Exhaustive logical-consistency and composition review across the seven normative spec pages.
- Pattern-matching against the public corpus of known ZK-SNARK and Bitcoin-protocol attack classes (frozen-heart, last-challenge, S2C nonce misuse, rogue-key, etc.).
- Per-property game-style argumentation grounded in the spec's clauses.
- A live sweep of the current attack landscape (IACR, Ethereum Foundation Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative, Cure53 NIP-44 audit, Least Authority Plonky2 audit) as of 2026-06.

**What I do not bring.**

- Independent cryptanalysis of Poseidon-Goldilocks at the algebraic level. I rely on the public state of that analysis (currently: full 8 full + 22 partial unbroken; reduced-round challenges of Phase 1 partially solved; Phase 2 KoalaBear track concludes Dec 2026).
- Independent soundness analysis of Plonky2 as a FRI-based recursive proof system. I rely on the public state (Least Authority audit 2022; ZKM research analysis; no public soundness breaks).
- Verification of a reference implementation. None exists in full-spec form yet; this review covers the spec-as-design.
- Accountability that an external auditor's signed report would carry. The risk-distribution effect of an external audit (insurance, reputation, legal accountability) is qualitative, not technical, and is something this review can *prepare* but not *substitute*.

**How to read confidence labels.**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | I have analysed the property directly; the argument composes cleanly from the spec's clauses; no known attack class applies; the underlying assumption is well-studied with no public weakness. |
| **Medium** | The property holds *modulo* a specific stated assumption whose public confidence is high but not maximal; or the argument has at least one composition step where I'd want a human cryptographer's second opinion before mainnet. |
| **I cannot assess** | The property depends on a primitive's intrinsic cryptanalytic strength against attacks I cannot independently evaluate (e.g. Plonky2 KS, Poseidon-Goldilocks against future algebraic attacks). I label the *composition* as sound under the assumption; the *assumption itself* is an explicit open dependence. |

A property labelled **High** here is *not* a substitute for an external review's stamp; it is my honest best assessment that, against the public corpus and the spec text, I find no defect.

## 1 · Cryptographic assumption inventory

The spec relies on the following primitives. Every property in §4 holds **conditional on** these assumptions; this table makes them explicit so the residual risk surface is one table, not scattered across narrative.

| ID | Assumption | Used by | Current public state |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Plonky2 KS** — knowledge-soundness of the FRI-based recursive proof system over Goldilocks with Poseidon | clause 1 (PCD), §2.3.3 step 2, every receive/verify path | Least Authority audit 2022; ZKM 2025 protocol analysis; no public soundness breaks. PCD with straightline knowledge-soundness composes without per-layer security loss ([IACR 2025/2037](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2037), [Springer 2024 "Security Bounds for PCD from Straightline Extractors"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-78017-2_16)) |
| A2 | **Plonky2 ZK** — zero-knowledge property of the same | clause 9, every privacy claim | Same status as A1; ZK is a property of the construction, not affected by the algebraic attack literature on Poseidon below |
| A3 | **Poseidon-Goldilocks collision resistance** (Plonky2 instance: state 12, rate 8, 8 full + 22 partial, x⁷ S-box, 118 S-boxes total) | every `Hc(...)` call, all SMT/Merkle roots, `asset_id`, `coin.identifier`, `nf`, `ash`, all per-tree leaves/nodes | **BBLP22 estimates ~95 bits of security against algebraic attacks**, below the 128-bit comfort target but well above any practical concern (2⁹⁵ work infeasible). [Ethereum Foundation Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative](https://www.poseidon-initiative.info/) (2024–2026, $130k bounty) systematically reduces round counts to find the breaking point; **Phase 1 challenges at 24/28/32 reduced rounds have been partially solved, but the full 8+22 configuration remains unbroken in any public attack** ([IACR 2025/954](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/954), [IACR 2025/1916](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1916), [IACR 2025/1893](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893)). See §5 R1 for the risk handling. |
| A4 | **Poseidon-Goldilocks sponge / domain-extension security** (rate-8 capacity-4 absorption) | every variable-length `Hc` input | Standard sponge security argument; capacity-4 gives ~128 bits against generic length-extension/2nd-PR. No public weakness on this specific instance. |
| A5 | **SHA-256 collision resistance** | `address = H(Pk₀)`, `name_hash = H(name)`, `H_tx`, `blob_id`, `invoice_message`, `grant_message`, `chal`, `chan_bind` | Standard. No public attack within meaningful margins. |
| A6 | **SHA-256 preimage resistance** | `address = H(Pk₀)` — the in-circuit `H(creator_pubkey) == owner` check binds `creator_pubkey` to its image | Standard. |
| A7 | **BIP-340 Schnorr EUF-CMA over secp256k1** | every `SpendRecord` signature; `op` grant signatures; `op` invoice signatures; ownership-proof signatures | Standard. BIP-340 is widely deployed (Taproot, Nov 2021). |
| A8 | **secp256k1 discrete log + ECDH** | per-coin shared secret `ss = ECDH(esk, IVPK)`, S2C tweak point arithmetic | Standard. |
| A9 | **HKDF-SHA-256 as PRF/KDF** | `K_tx`, `dk` | Standard. |
| A10 | **NIP-44 v2 IND-CCA** (ChaCha20 + HMAC-SHA-256 over ECDH-derived key) | every bundle ciphertext, every delivery event, every ACK | Audited by Cure53 in Dec 2023; no published vulnerabilities. |
| A11 | **NIP-59 gift-wrap** — sender/recipient indistinguishability of the outer envelope | delivery event metadata privacy | Used unchanged from Nostr; the relay sees an opaque event addressed to an ephemeral key. |
| A12 | **Bitcoin honest-majority hashpower & no reorgs ≥ 6 blocks** | finality at 6 confirmations (§3.9); `completed` as absolute (§3.10); accumulator stability across the inclusion-block depth | Industry default. Spec explicitly treats deeper reorg as protocol-failure event (§3.10, §6.6). |
| A13 | **Bitcoin per-node eclipse resistance** (operator runs own `bitcoind`) | scanner has true chain view | Same assumption as any Bitcoin application; spec acknowledges in §6.6. |
| A14 | **TLS 1.2 + EMS, or TLS 1.3, or Tor v3** for the pull endpoint | host-binding of `chan_bind` (§5.1); MITM resistance against `OwnershipProof` forwarding | Standard, spec is explicit about minimum versions. |

**Stability of assumptions.** A1, A2, A3, A4 are zkCoins-specific in the sense that protocol version v1 fixes a concrete instance. If, during the v1 lifecycle, A3 is weakened by a future algebraic attack (the Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative is *designed* to find the boundary), the spec's version-bump mechanism (`"zkCoins/v1/..."` namespace, §1.1; v2 issuance branch, §6.5) is the planned exit path.

## 2 · Adversary model

For every property in §4, the adversary is **probabilistic polynomial-time** and **may**:

- Run arbitrary numbers of users, accounts, addresses, and seeds.
- Run an arbitrary number of publishers; control which records they publish, in which order, with which `block_anchor` choices.
- Run an arbitrary number of nodes (relays + scanners); control their delivery, retention, and pull responses; control all answers they return to delegating wallets.
- Control all Nostr relays the victim queries (except as constrained by §4.6 replication).
- Observe Bitcoin in full; submit Bitcoin transactions.
- Mount network attacks on victim ↔ peer-node connections, subject to A14.
- Mount arbitrary computation up to a security parameter (typically 2¹²⁸ work).

The adversary **may not**:

- Break A1–A14 (those are the explicit dependencies).
- Compromise the victim's wallet (SPEND branch); this is the protocol's one trust root.
- Mount a Bitcoin-consensus break (51% attack with deep reorg, ≥ 6 blocks; A12).

Where a property's argument requires a sharper adversary restriction, the section states it.

## 3 · Methodology

For each property P, the argument proceeds:

1. **Statement** — what the protocol promises (mapped to a numbered Requirement where applicable).
2. **Game / scenario** — what the adversary must achieve to violate it.
3. **Attack-class enumeration** — every public class of attack I have considered, with a sentence on why it does or does not apply.
4. **Verdict + confidence label** — the assessment.

Where a property depends on a still-open spec finding (F-tracker), the dependence is stated and the assessment is conditional.

## 4 · Properties under review

### P1 · No-Forgery (mints excluded; covered by P7)

**Statement.** A coin `c` with `c.recipient = R`, `c.amount = v`, `c.asset_id = a` can exist only as the proven output of a §2.1-compliant transition signed by some account's `skᵢ`. A PPT adversary without `skᵢ` for that account, and without breaking A1, A5, A7, cannot cause an honest receiver to credit `c`.

**Game.** A wins if there exists a coin `c` that an honest receiver credits, but `c` was not produced by a valid §2.1 transition.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Direct forgery of the validity proof.* Requires breaking A1 (Plonky2 KS). Out.
- *Forgery of the `SpendRecord` signature.* Requires breaking A7. Out.
- *Forgery of `H(Pk₀) == owner` in clause 2.* SHA-256 preimage (A6); out.
- *Substitution of a different `Pkᵢ` in clause 2 (the transition pubkey check).* The clause checks `txn_pubkey == prev_account_state.current_pubkey`, with `prev_account_state` recursive-bound to `ash` via clause 1. Wouldn't compose without breaking A1.
- *Recomputation of `coin.identifier` for a forged coin.* Clause 2(c) recomputes `Hc("Coin", input_auth[j].creating_prev_ash ‖ asset_id ‖ coin_index)`. A different `creating_prev_ash` (faking provenance) collides with the supplied identifier only via Poseidon collision (A3).
- *Bypass via "presence" vs "admission" anchor-check.* This was [F6](./2026-06-06.02.md#f6--present-vs-admitted-spendrecord-️) (HIGH); resolved by `state == completed` gate at all four receive sites (PR [#25](https://github.com/zk-coins/docs/pull/25)).
- *Frozen-Heart-class attack on Fiat-Shamir.* Clause 9 of §2.1 ("public-input binding") names all four `ProofData` fields explicitly and requires them to be the in-circuit-computed values; the [Trail of Bits Frozen-Heart pattern](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2022/04/18/the-frozen-heart-vulnerability-in-plonk/) (omitting public inputs from the transcript) is structurally prevented by the explicit binding rule. Plonky2's implementation includes public inputs in its transcript; not a spec-level defect.
- *Last-Challenge / [OpenZeppelin Linea-style](https://blog.openzeppelin.com/the-last-challenge-attack) Fiat-Shamir misuse.* Same structural defense: clause 9 fixes the public-input set, and Plonky2's Fiat-Shamir is the well-vetted hashing of the full transcript. Not a spec-level concern but flagged below as an implementation-review item (R3).
- *Sign-to-contract nonce malleability.* §3.2 specifies the canonical S2C construction; the published signature is an ordinary BIP-340 signature whose nonce `R` is `R' + t·G`. A malleability that allowed `(R, s)` and `(R', s')` over distinct `ProofData` to both verify would require breaking either BIP-340 EUF-CMA or the SHA-256 collision resistance of `t = H(R' ‖ H_tx)`. Out under A5, A7.
- *Asset-id collision across creators.* Requires a Poseidon collision on the 5-tuple (genesis_tag, creator_pubkey, name_hash, decimals, issuance_version). A3.

**Verdict.** Sound under A1, A3, A5, A6, A7. **Confidence: HIGH** for the spec design. A real implementation must additionally instantiate Plonky2 correctly (no per-implementation Frozen-Heart-style transcript bugs) — that is a reference-implementation review item, not a spec-level concern.

### P2 · No-Double-Spend

**Statement.** Each coin `c` produced by the protocol can be successfully spent at most once. A PPT adversary cannot cause two distinct honest receivers to credit two distinct downstream coins that both consume `c`.

**Game.** A wins if two receivers R₁, R₂ are credited coins c₁, c₂ such that both transitions consumed `c` (i.e. both proofs name `nf_c = Hc("Nullifier", nk ‖ c.identifier)`).

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Both records get admitted on-chain.* §3.6 step 6 (first-spend-wins, canonical order) rejects the second occurrence of `nf_c`. Both R₁ and R₂'s receive-side step 5 (§2.3.3) checks non-membership; the one whose creating `SpendRecord` was admitted second sees `nf_c` already present and rejects. **One** receiver credits, not two.
- *One record `SR1` admitted, second `SR2` rejected; R₂ gets the bundle from `SR2`.* §3.10 receive predicate requires `SR2.state == completed`. `SR2` is `failed` under §3.10. R₂ rejects.
- *`SR1` is at `pending` (< 6 conf), `SR2` is also published in a competing fork (post-reorg scenario).* §3.7 reorg handling removes orphaned-block `nf`s and recomputes the accumulator for the new tip. The eventual canonical tip resolves which is `completed`. Under A12 (reorg ≤ 5 blocks), `completed` is absolute.
- *Pure off-chain double-delivery (deliver bundle to R₂ without ever spending against them).* If the spender never publishes `SR2`, only `SR1` exists; `nf_c` ∈ accumulator. R₂'s step 5 (compute `nf` for the *receive coin*, not the consumed one) checks the **receive coin's** nf is not present. Subtle: the receive coin `c₂` has its **own** `coin.identifier` and the receiver computes `nf_{c₂}` from its own `nk`. The check is for the receive coin being unspent, not for the consumed coin being still unspent. Wait — let me re-read §2.3.3 step 5: *"verify the coin's own `nf` is NOT among them — i.e. the coin is unspent."* "The coin" here is the **received** coin (c₂), not the input coin (c). So step 5 doesn't catch double-delivery directly. **But** step 2 (full recursive verify) attests that `c₂` was produced by a valid transition; step 4 (anchor) requires that transition's `SR1` to be `completed`. If `SR2` (the second consumption of c) was never published, the second downstream lineage doesn't exist as a coin at all — it would require a second `SpendRecord` to bind `c₂`'s `ocr`. So either (a) the spender publishes only `SR1` and c₂ doesn't exist as a credible coin (no `ocr` ↔ `c₂` binding on-chain), or (b) the spender publishes `SR2` and either it admits (and then R₁ or R₂ loses by first-spend-wins) or it fails (and the §3.10 gate blocks credit). Either way, R₁ and R₂ cannot both credit. Confirmed sound.
- *Same `nf` published in two records within the SAME inscription batch (same block, same tx).* Canonical order by `j` within the batch (§3.6 step 5 tertiary key). First wins. Second rejected.
- *Within-record duplicate `nf` (k_j > 1, same nf listed twice).* See **F16** in §6 — spec language for rollback semantics is ambiguous in this edge case, but the worst outcome is the attacker burning their own coin (self-DoS), not theft.
- *Pre-mining a colliding `nf` (different account, different coin, same nf hash).* Requires Poseidon collision (A3) on `Hc("Nullifier", nk ‖ id)`.
- *Race: courier delays the second receiver's accumulator view.* Step 5 is anchored to a stated tip; an out-of-date tip is self-identifying (§3.7) and receivers MUST re-evaluate. A delayed view becomes correct as soon as it catches up.

**Verdict.** Sound under A3, A12. **Confidence: HIGH**. Subject to F16 wording tightening (cosmetic; no security impact in worst case).

### P3 · Per-Asset Balance Conservation

**Statement.** For every asset `a`, the total credited supply across all honest receivers equals the total `Mint(a)` produced by valid issuance transitions, minus any retained-by-issuer change.

**Game.** A wins if the cross-receiver sum of credited `a`-coin amounts exceeds the sum of `Mint(a)` amounts over admitted v1 issuance transitions.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Direct forgery of a coin's `asset_id`.* See P1.
- *Bypassing clause 3 (`In(a) + Mint(a) ≥ Out(a)`) via field-arithmetic overflow.* §1.7.3 mandates `amount` is range-checked in-circuit to `[0, 2^128 − 1]`. With sums of bounded amounts (typical transition has O(few) inputs and outputs), worst-case sum fits in u160 or so, far below the Goldilocks prime (~2⁶⁴) — wait, Goldilocks is 64-bit. **Important check:** can a sum of amounts wrap the field?

  Each `amount ∈ [0, 2¹²⁸ − 1]`. Goldilocks `p ≈ 2⁶⁴ − 2³² + 1`. Native field elements can't hold a u128 directly; the §1.7.2 wide-numeric rule absorbs amounts as byte-strings (3 limbs of 7 bytes for u128). But the **in-circuit arithmetic for balance conservation** isn't specified at the field-decomposition level here — the spec mandates the range-check and conservation predicate; the implementation must decompose u128 into multi-limb arithmetic.

  This is a real implementation discipline (multi-limb u128 ops in a 64-bit field), but the spec mandate ("range-checked in-circuit, sums never wrap the field") is the right rule. **Implementation review item, not a spec defect.**

- *Two issuance transitions with the same `asset_id` and inconsistent supply accounting.* v1 has no protocol-level cap (§6.5). A creator can mint any amount any number of times. **By design**. Holders' protection is the social/economic discipline that the creator is a known entity, not protocol math. Documented in §6.5 ("Supply discipline is a creator's commitment, not a protocol guarantee").
- *Fake issuance under a creator's identity.* Clause 2 of v1 mint requires `H(creator_pubkey) == prev_account_state.owner`, where `creator_pubkey` is witnessed. To produce a v1 mint as creator C, the prover needs `sk₀` of C (to sign the SpendRecord). A6 + A7.
- *Account-level equivocation by the creator (signing two parallel mint histories).* §6.5 makes this **publicly observable on Bitcoin** (rotating `Pkᵢ` would appear in two distinct `SpendRecord`s for the creator's account). It is **not** prevented at the protocol level; holders detect it by chain observation. Documented design tradeoff.

**Verdict.** Sound under A1, A3, A6, A7 **plus** the explicit v1 model where supply is creator-bound, not protocol-bound. **Confidence: HIGH for the design as stated.** The reader must understand that v1 deliberately omits protocol-enforced supply caps (§6.5 names this; future versions will reintroduce them). If "supply conservation" is the wrong frame and "supply discipline" is the right one, the protocol delivers exactly the latter.

### P4 · Zero-Knowledge (witness confidentiality)

**Statement.** A proof π produced under §2.1 reveals to a verifier only the four `ProofData` fields (all roots/digests) and no information about the witness — amounts, asset ids, recipients, keys, counts, the coin graph.

**Game.** A wins if there exists a PPT distinguisher that, given two witnesses w₀, w₁ producing the same `ProofData`, can distinguish π₀ from π₁ with non-negligible advantage.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Plonky2 ZK property violation.* A2 dependency.
- *Leakage through the public-input set itself.* The roots `output_coins_root`, `input_nullifiers_root`, `coin_history_root`, `new_account_state_hash` are 32-byte Poseidon digests. Distinct witnesses with the same `ProofData` are by construction indistinguishable at the public-input level.
- *Side-channel via proof size or timing.* Plonky2 produces constant-size proofs (§2.2). Constant-time verification. No size/timing leak.
- *Cross-transition correlation (rotating `Pkᵢ`).* Each transition's signature uses a fresh `Pkᵢ` derived from BIP-32 hardened path. Without `nk` and the spend branch (held only by the wallet), an outside observer cannot link two `SpendRecord`s of the same account. Anonymity-set quality depends on usage density (acknowledged in `risks.md`).
- *Cross-protocol composition with NIP-44 envelope leakage.* The bundle ciphertext is NIP-44 v2 with power-of-2 padding (audited Dec 2023). Padding leaks an upper bound on bundle size — bundles are ~100KB+ with very similar size, so no amount-discriminating leak.

**Verdict.** Sound under A2, A10. **Confidence: HIGH**.

### P5 · On-chain Privacy / Unlinkability

**Statement.** A passive observer of Bitcoin learns from a `SpendRecord` only: that **some** zkCoins transition occurred, the input-count `kⱼ` (acknowledged metadata leak per §3.5), and the rotating `Pkᵢ` plus the spent `nf`s — but cannot recover the owner address, the amount, the asset, the recipient, or link to any other `SpendRecord` of the same account.

**Game.** A wins if it can, with non-negligible probability, output a partition of admitted `SpendRecord`s such that each partition class corresponds exactly to one account.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Direct linkage via `Pkᵢ`.* Each transition uses a fresh hardened-derived `Pkᵢ`; without the seed, no key path can be reconstructed. BIP-32 hardened derivation security depends on HKDF-SHA512 (BIP-32 KDF) PRF property — out under A9 (closely related primitive).
- *Direct linkage via `nf`.* `nf = Hc("Nullifier", nk ‖ identifier)`. Without `nk` (SPEND branch), `nf` is indistinguishable from a random 256-bit digest. A3 + A2 (the `nk` is never revealed in any public input).
- *Linkage via `inr` / `ocr`.* Hashes of nullifier and coin sets; preimage-resistant under A3.
- *Linkage via `k_j` (input-count leak).* This is the **accepted, documented leak** (§3.5). Spending the same N inputs repeatedly is observable as a same-N pattern; a wallet wanting to blunt it can split or pad. The spec doesn't claim hide it; the privacy bound stated is exactly the bound delivered.
- *Linkage via inscription envelope metadata.* Marker `0x42 0x42` identifies the protocol but says nothing about the account. Taproot reveal Tx data is generic.
- *Linkage via publisher behaviour (timing, batching pattern).* A naïve self-publisher exposes a UTXO. The §3.8 "broadcaster-paid" model (publisher pays the BTC fee, recouped via off-chain transfer) addresses this; the spec calls it MAY rather than MUST. **Documented privacy guidance** but the user-side privacy of a self-publishing operation depends on how the user funds it.
- *Cross-protocol fingerprinting (NIP-44 padding, NIP-59 envelope, relay choice).* Spec §4.7 documents these. Operating behind Tor mitigates network-level. Cover traffic from cohabiting Nostr events helps.
- *Long-run intersection attacks against small anonymity sets.* Acknowledged in `risks.md`; standard for any privacy protocol; mitigated by usage density.

**Verdict.** Sound for the **stated** privacy bound (on-chain hides amount, asset, parties, graph; leaks input-count). **Confidence: HIGH for on-chain unlinkability**, **MEDIUM** for end-to-end network-layer privacy (which depends on operational hygiene the spec calls out but cannot enforce).

### P6 · Client-Side Validation (no third-party trust)

**Statement.** A receiver crediting a coin depends only on (a) the Bitcoin chain it itself reads, (b) the bundle it received, (c) its own derivable keys. No node, courier, sender, or third party can cause an honest receiver to credit a coin that fails any of §2.3.3 steps 2–6.

**Game.** A wins if it can cause an honest receiver — running §2.3.3 correctly — to credit a coin that is not produced by a valid transition admitted on Bitcoin.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Subverting step 2 (recursive verify).* Requires breaking A1.
- *Subverting step 3 (inclusion in `ocr`).* Requires breaking A3.
- *Subverting step 4 (anchor in a `completed` SpendRecord).* `completed` requires `admitted` (§3.5+§3.6) + ≥ 6 confirmations. F6 resolved this previously. To subvert: would require either bypassing admission (rejected by P1) or persuading the receiver that ≥ 6 confirmations exist when they don't (subverts A13 — the receiver's own Bitcoin view).
- *Subverting step 5 (nf non-membership).* `nf` is computed in-receiver from its own `nk`. The accumulator is rebuilt in-receiver from the chain. Bypassing requires either breaking A3, or persuading the receiver's chain view (A13).
- *Subverting step 6 (amount/asset sanity).* `coin.recipient == receiver.address` and `asset_id` well-formed are local checks.
- *Compromising the node that delivers the bundle.* The node can withhold (liveness) but cannot forge — every bundle is verified end-to-end. Documented design.
- *Compromising the relay set.* §4.6 replication factor `k = 3` over **independent** operators; the receiver MUST eventually reach one. Liveness, not safety.

**Verdict.** Sound under A1, A3, A12, A13. **Confidence: HIGH** for the protocol property; the spec correctly identifies that operator-network-level eclipse is an inherited Bitcoin assumption (A13) the spec cannot improve over.

### P7 · Issuance Authenticity (v1)

**Statement.** A coin claiming asset `a` can be admitted as a v1-issuance output only by a transition signed by the creator account of `a` (the account whose `Pk₀` satisfies `Hc("AssetId", genesis_tag ‖ Pk₀ ‖ H(name) ‖ decimals ‖ 1) == a`).

**Game.** A wins if a coin with asset `a` is admitted via the v1 mint clauses but the signing account is not the creator account.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Fake `creator_pubkey` witness.* Clause (b) of v1 mint requires `H(creator_pubkey) == prev_account_state.owner`. The transition's `txn_sig` is BIP-340 by `Pkᵢ = prev_account_state.current_pubkey`. To make this work, the adversary needs both: (i) a `creator_pubkey` whose SHA-256 equals `owner`; (ii) the spend key to sign as the current spender of that account. (i) is SHA-256 preimage (A6); (ii) is A7.
- *Discrepancy between `creator_pubkey` (witnessed) and `Pkᵢ` (signed under).* `creator_pubkey` is `Pk₀` (the initial spend key), `Pkᵢ` is the *current* per-transition key. The clause `H(creator_pubkey) == owner` binds Pk₀ to the address; clause 2 of §2.1 binds `Pkᵢ` to `prev_account_state.current_pubkey`. The recursive proof's clause 1 attests `prev_account_state.owner == initial owner == H(Pk₀)` (since clause 7 says `new_account_state.owner` MUST be unchanged). So `creator_pubkey` is forced to be the **same Pk₀** that fixed the account at creation, and the account itself must possess the current SPEND key path to mint. Composition holds.
- *Replay of a v1 mint as v2.* `asset_id` binds `issuance_version`. A v2 mint of the same `asset_id` is structurally a different `asset_id` (different version → different digest under A3). A v1 coin cannot be claimed v2; a v2 coin cannot be claimed v1.
- *Cap evasion (v1 has no cap).* By design; documented in §6.5.

**Verdict.** Sound under A1, A3, A6, A7. **Confidence: HIGH** for v1.

The architectural commitment to **single-circuit version-branching** for future versions (§6.5 "Forward compatibility") correctly identifies the cyclic-recursion constraint and avoids the per-version-circuit cul-de-sac. This is a design-level future-proofing decision and is the right one.

### P8 · Transport Confidentiality + Authentication

**Statement.** The off-chain delivery channel reveals to a relay (or a passive eavesdropper) only that an opaque, gift-wrapped, NIP-44-encrypted event was stored at some time; the recipient receives a bundle if and only if a valid sender produced it; ACKs cannot be forged.

**Game (confidentiality).** A relay-controlling adversary distinguishes the content of two delivery events with the same outer envelope.
**Game (authentication).** An adversary produces a bundle that the recipient's §4.5 verification accepts as valid.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Confidentiality.* Bundle is NIP-44 v2 over per-coin `K_tx` (derived from ECDH(ivk, epk), A8 + A9 + A10). Delivery event payload is NIP-44 to `IVPK`, then NIP-59 gift-wrapped. A10 + A11.
- *Authentication.* Bundle content is verified end-to-end via §4.5 — proof + chain checks. A bundle that decrypts but fails verification is dropped. No transport-level signature is needed because the validity proof is its own authentication.
- *ACK forgery.* ACK is `op`-signed (BIP-340; A7). Recipient's `op_pubkey` is published in the invoice (signed by `op`, recursively rooted in the address publication path). An adversary without the recipient's `op` secret cannot forge a valid ACK; the sender's retention logic (§4.2) protects the bundle until ACK + replication confirmed.
- *Replay of an old delivery event.* The bundle is content-addressed; storing it again is a no-op. Replay of an old ACK could fool the sender into early-dropping a bundle. **Subtle**: the spec says "the sender's node MUST return an acknowledgement … carrying detect_tag and blob_id and signed by recipient's op." This is **not nonce-bound** — an ACK valid once is valid forever for that (detect_tag, blob_id) pair. If the same recipient later requests retransmission (e.g. after data loss), the original ACK is still valid; but the original-bundle drop already happened. **Sub-finding (LOW)**: ACK is content-addressed (good for idempotence) but not freshness-bound. A relay that captured one ACK and reuses it on a later, NEW delivery of the same bundle would falsely tell the sender it can drop — but this requires the same (detect_tag, blob_id) which is per-coin and identifies the same coin. Practical exploit window is narrow. Flagged as F17 for completeness.
- *Detect-tag based linkability.* Per-coin fresh `epk` makes tags all-distinct (§1.3, §4.4). Sound.

**Verdict.** Sound under A8, A9, A10, A11. **Confidence: HIGH** with minor F17 (LOW) on ACK freshness wording.

---

### P9 · Recovery Completeness

**Statement.** From seed + Bitcoin + the §4.6 replicated bundles, a wallet rebuilds its spendable state without trusting any node.

**Game.** A wins if the recovery procedure (§4.5) terminates accepting a coin the seed-controlled account did not actually receive, or rejecting a coin it did.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *False accept.* §4.5 step 4 mandates independent verification of every returned bundle against Bitcoin. A node returning a forged bundle fails this check.
- *False reject.* A bundle correctly delivered will verify under §4.5; the only failure mode is **unavailability** (no replica returns it). Liveness, not safety. Replication `k ≥ 2` (default 3) on independent operators makes this an availability-not-safety problem.
- *Selective censorship by the network.* Multiple independent nodes; if all collude, the user is stuck. §4.6 says default `k = 3`; deployments MAY raise. Spec is honest about this being availability, not safety.
- *Stale balance reconstruction.* Step 5 uses the **bundle** to reconstruct balances (`AccountState`) since `balances` is off-chain. Self-delivery rule of §4.2 (new in develop) is **the** mechanism that makes this work. Without self-delivery, the wallet would see "my account spent" (from chain) but not "the resulting balance." Self-delivery rule is correct and necessary.

**Verdict.** Sound for the **stated** trust model (correctness from chain + verifying primitives; availability from replication). **Confidence: HIGH for correctness**, **MEDIUM for liveness** (depends on `k` and operator independence, which the spec is explicit about).

### P10 · Capability Discipline (Access & Explorer)

**Statement.** A presented capability releases at most the data its scope authorizes; no capability widens spend authority; bearer capabilities (`zkview`, `zkavk`) decrypt only what their key allows; `OwnershipProof` and `GrantProof` cannot be replayed against a different node.

**Game.** A wins if it can cause a node to release Private data beyond a presented capability's scope, or replay a captured `OwnershipProof` against a different node, or escalate a view capability into a spend.

**Attack-class enumeration.**

- *Scope evasion.* §5.1 mandates the node enforces scope; this is implementation discipline. Spec is clear.
- *Replay against a different node ("proof forwarding MITM").* `chan_bind` binds the proof to the host the requester dialed. PR [#22](https://github.com/zk-coins/docs/pull/22) (`4c1eb8c`) hardened this exact attack class. Sound.
- *Replay against the same node.* Server-issued nonce + expiry, MUST-consumed-once. Sound.
- *Spend escalation from view capability.* `ivk`/`ovk`/`op` are hardened siblings of SPEND in BIP-32; cannot derive SPEND. A9 (BIP-32 PRF).
- *`zkavk` bearer leakage.* `zkavk` is bearer-irrevocable; spec explicitly says rotation requires moving to a new account, and recommends `zkgrant` for retractable scope. Documented tradeoff.
- *Per-coin `zkview` leakage.* Decrypts exactly one coin; symmetric note key; cannot derive other coins' keys. Sound.

**Verdict.** Sound under A7, A9. **Confidence: HIGH**.

## 6 · Findings introduced in this pass

### F16 — Within-record duplicate-`nf` rollback semantics (LOW)

**Where.** [`onchain.md` §3.6 step 6](https://github.com/zk-coins/docs/blob/develop/docs/specification/onchain.md#36-chain-scanning).

**The wording.** *"In that order, for each record, insert each published `nf` into the global nullifier accumulator. If an `nf` is **already present**, that record is a double-spend attempt: the scanner MUST treat the whole record as **invalid** — its `nf`s are not re-inserted and its output coins are treated as never created."*

**The edge case.** A maliciously-constructed `SpendRecord` with `nullifiersⱼ = [nf_A, nf_A]` (same `nf` listed twice within the same record). The on-chain admission scan does **not** verify the §2.1 validity proof (the validity proof is off-chain); it only verifies the BIP-340 signature and the nullifier-`inr` binding. A holder with `skᵢ` and a known `nf_A` could craft and sign such a record.

When the scanner processes it:

1. j = 0 of the record: `nf_A` is inserted (assume it was absent prior).
2. j = 1 of the same record: `nf_A` is already present (from j = 0) → "record is a double-spend attempt" → rollback rule fires.

**Ambiguity.** The wording *"its `nf`s are not re-inserted"* is read by an honest implementer two ways:

- **(R1, intended)** Reverse the partial insertion: `nf_A` is removed from the accumulator; the record is treated as if no `nf` was ever published.
- **(R2, literal)** Only the *not-yet-inserted* nullifiers are suppressed; `nf_A` (already inserted at j = 0) stays in the accumulator.

Two honest implementers could reach different accumulator states for this record. Under the verdict-lens criterion (Pass 2), that is a finding.

**Impact.**
- **Theft surface: none.** Under either interpretation, the attacker burns their own `nf_A` (either it stays in the accumulator under R2, blocking any future spend; or it doesn't, but the attacker's record is already marked invalid so no value flows). The attacker self-DoSes by burning their nullifier; nobody else loses money.
- **Implementation-divergence surface.** Two honest scanners would disagree on whether `nf_A` is in the accumulator after this block, which would cascade into divergent non-membership answers for future receives. This is the real concern: **consensus** of the rebuilt accumulator across honest nodes is the foundation of trustless receive (P6).

**Fix (LOW, wording).** Pin the rollback semantics explicitly. Recommended language: *"If, during this record's processing, an `nf` is found already present in the accumulator, the scanner MUST treat the whole record as invalid: **any `nf`s of this record that have already been inserted during its processing MUST be removed**, and its output coins are treated as never created. The accumulator state after the record's processing MUST be identical to its state immediately before."* This forces R1 unambiguously.

**Severity.** LOW. No theft; pure implementation-divergence prevention. A clarification, not a structural change.

### F17 — ACK freshness is not nonce-bound (LOW)

**Where.** [`transport-recovery.md` §4.2 ACK + retry](https://github.com/zk-coins/docs/blob/develop/docs/specification/transport-recovery.md#42-bundle-delivery).

**The wording.** *"On successful receipt and verification (§4.5), the recipient's node MUST return an acknowledgement: a NIP-44-encrypted, NIP-59 gift-wrapped event addressed back to the sender, carrying `detect_tag` and `blob_id` and signed by the recipient's `op`."*

**The observation.** An ACK is bound to `(detect_tag, blob_id)` and signed by `op`. It is **not** bound to a sender-chosen nonce or to a time window. A captured ACK is replayable indefinitely against the same sender for the same `(detect_tag, blob_id)`.

**Scenario.** A relay that observed an ACK in the past stores it. The same recipient later loses local state and re-requests the same bundle to be re-delivered (sender keeps a copy if the user has multiple devices, or after relay-replication failure on a separate channel). The relay replays the old ACK to the sender. The sender, having received "a valid ACK," may drop its retained copy too early, **before** the recipient has actually re-received this round's delivery.

**Severity.** LOW. The sender's drop rule (§4.2) requires *both* a valid ACK *and* confirmed `k`-replication; the replication requirement is the operative safety latch. An attacker controlling all `k` replicas defeats availability anyway (out of scope per A12/A13-style "≥ 1 honest peer" reasoning). Hence the practical exploit window is narrow.

**Fix (LOW, wording).** Either (a) bind the ACK to a sender-chosen nonce echoed in the delivery event (preferred — costs one 32-byte field, no protocol change), or (b) clarify in §4.2 that the sender's drop rule's safety depends on the `k`-replication check and an ACK alone is insufficient.

## 9 · Recommendations

**M1 (must-do, deployment-blocker)** — Resolve [F15](#f1-f15-f9-f12--carry-forward-status). The reference Plonky2 circuit, its gate set, its public-input order, and the resulting verifier-data artefact must be designated as the **canonical** version, with a hash inscribed in the spec, before any mainnet deployment.

**M2 (must-do, deployment-blocker)** — Produce the test vectors currently marked `<REGEN>` ([`test-vectors.md`](https://github.com/zk-coins/docs/blob/develop/docs/specification/test-vectors.md)) so cross-implementation conformance can be checked.

**M3 (should-do, hygiene)** — Address F16, F17, F1 with the wording fixes named in §6. None are blockers; all are clarity improvements that strengthen verdict-lens consensus.

**M4 (should-do, ongoing)** — Track the Ethereum Foundation Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative results through Dec 2026. If the margin erodes meaningfully, accelerate the Plonky3/Poseidon2 migration path the project already has in its ROADMAP.

**M5 (recommended, risk-distribution)** — Even with this internal review at the rigor demonstrated above, the user is making a judgement that **my confidence is enough** for their risk tolerance, not that an independent verifier has signed off. For a system with real money at scale, the residual R1–R4 are the items where a second, external, cryptographer-led review carries qualitative value (accountability, insurance, attack-surface diversification) that this internal review cannot substitute. This is presented as information for the user's decision, not as a refusal to do the review.

## 10 · Verdict

**The zkCoins v1 specification, as of [`01ae663`](https://github.com/zk-coins/docs/commit/01ae663), composes soundly against my full analysis of the public attack-class corpus and the spec's own clause structure.**

No property in §4 falls below MEDIUM at the spec design level, and the MEDIUMs are exclusively where the spec correctly identifies that a guarantee depends on operational discipline outside the protocol's control. Two new LOW findings (F16, F17) are wording clarifications, not structural defects.

The structural deployment-blocker remains **F15** (canonical verifier-data designation), which is gated on the reference implementation existing and being itself reviewed — a step this document explicitly **prepares for** but does not **constitute**.

The honest residual-risk surface (§8) is concentrated in R1 (Poseidon-Goldilocks long-tail algebraic-attack risk, narrowing through Dec 2026) and R3 (reference-implementation review). Both are addressable through the project's existing roadmap.
