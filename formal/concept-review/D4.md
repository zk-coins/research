# D4 — Liveness and Incentive Compatibility

> Concept-viability dimension D4. The safety properties P1–P10 establish what
> *cannot happen* (no theft, no forgery, no double-spend, no false-accept on
> recovery). This dimension asks the orthogonal question: **what guarantees
> progress, and do rational, self-interested actors actually play the
> spec-conformant strategy?** Safety is necessary but not sufficient — a
> protocol can be perfectly safe and still grind to a halt, centralize, or
> under-provision the very resources its safety proofs assume are available.

## Methodology

This review is **game-theoretic, not cryptographic**. For each actor we
enumerate:

1. the **honest strategy** and its cost;
2. the **deviation strategies** available without breaking any cryptographic
   rule (i.e. deviations the soundness proofs do *not* forbid);
3. the **payoff** of deviating — is it *profitable* (attacker gains value) or
   merely *possible* (griefing: attacker burns value to impose cost on others);
4. **what the protocol does about it** (the spec's stated answer); and
5. the **residual** — what is left unhandled after the spec's answer.

We then predict the **equilibrium**: with rational actors, does the network
converge to spec-conformant behaviour, to professionalized centralization, or
to systematic under-provision?

**Scope.** This analysis covers the **core zkCoins protocol** as specified in
`docs/docs/specification.md` (permissionless publisher, node-as-relay mesh,
nullifier accumulator). It does **not** cover the SSS / venue / perpetuals
"sidesystem" design in `notes/threat-model.md` and `notes/l2-protocol-design.md`
(Mode A/B assets, bonded SSS attestation, force-exit) — that is a distinct
construction with its own bonded-actor incentive model and is out of scope for
the core-protocol liveness question. Where the threat-model's core-relevant
attacks map cleanly (S5 fee front-running, M1/M2 miner bribery) they are cited.

**Ground truth on what is *proven*.** Of the formally verified properties, only
P9 (Recovery Completeness) touches liveness, and the certificate is explicit
that **the liveness half is NOT in the unbounded certificate**:

- `formal/CERTIFICATE.md`: P9 is "verified: safety unbounded; liveness via
  enabledness surrogate"; full temporal liveness "not certifiable in Apalache
  0.58.0 (no fairness support — the genuine `WF ⇒ ◇□` form throws
  `NotImplementedError`)".
- `formal/property/P09_RecoveryCompleteness/notes.md`: the team *ran* the
  fairness-free temporal form `<>[](all recovered)` and it returns a
  **counterexample** — "without fairness the untrusted network can withhold
  forever (a pure stutter/withholding trace), so the bundle is never
  recovered." The certificate carries only an **enabledness surrogate**
  ("recovery *can* always make progress … NOT that it eventually *does*"). The
  liveness grade is **MEDIUM**, "contingent on `k`, operator independence, and a
  reachable honest replica."

**This is the headline finding restated formally:** the protocol's own
verification artefacts confirm that *nothing in the proof base guarantees
progress*. Liveness is assumed (an honest, reachable, willing replica/publisher
exists), never demonstrated — and the entire D4 question is whether rational
actors make that assumption hold. The actor-by-actor analysis below answers:
for several roles, **no**.

## Actor-by-actor analysis

### 1. Publisher (permissionless aggregator)

**Honest strategy & cost.** Collect `m` `SpendRecord`s, build one recursive
`AggregateBatchProof` (~100 KB, proving time sub-linear in `m` but still heavy —
a per-record in-circuit signature check plus SMT-insertion folded into a
recursive PCD proof), disperse the `BatchBundle` to `k−1 = 2` independent
replicas, broadcast a 231-byte Taproot inscription (~$0.58 on-chain at 10
sat/vB, $100k BTC), then **retain and serve the bundle indefinitely** (§4.6
long-term-retention rule). The publisher is *not* trusted for correctness or
custody (§3.4) — a bad proof is simply rejected.

**Revenue.** The only revenue path is the **broadcaster-paid MAY-compensation**
(§3.8): the spender *MAY* embed a publisher-fee allowance in the off-chain
settlement portion of the `CoinProof` bundle, which the publisher claims as a
zkCoins-native transfer. Crucially, the source construction (shieldedcsv-paper.pdf,
"payment finalize fee" predicate) makes this claimable **"given they are the
first to publish the nullifier on the blockchain."** The fee is contingent on
*winning the inclusion race*.

**Deviation strategies.**

- **D1.1 Withhold / censor** a specific `SpendRecord` (refuse to include it).
  Pure griefing against one spender — costs the publisher nothing but forgoes
  one fee. Spec answer: permissionless, submit elsewhere. *Profitable?* No
  (forgoes fee), but free to do. See attack catalogue R-D4-1.
- **D1.2 Fee-snipe / chain-bid every tip** (sequential-prev_root monopoly).
  *Profitable* against the spender population (extracts fee/ordering control)
  and *griefs* rival publishers. See §2 and R-D4-2/R-D4-3.
- **D1.3 Drop the bundle after the inscription is admitted** (free-ride on the
  §4.6 scanner-retention rule: let the honest scanner network store it for
  free). *Profitable* — saves storage/bandwidth. See R-D4-7.
- **D1.4 Set extractive fees.** Spec answer: fee policy is not consensus, use
  another publisher or self-publish (§3.8). Real only if the competitive/
  self-publish fallback is real — see §1 self-publish analysis below.

**The self-publish escape, examined honestly.** §3.4 and §3.8 repeatedly answer
censorship/rent with "anyone can self-publish." Post-`docs#40`, self-publishing
is **not** broadcasting a cheap 64-byte nullifier — it requires the spender to:
(a) build a full `AggregateBatchProof` (recursive PCD, ~100 KB, heavy proving —
the spec itself calls the publisher "computationally heavier than a plain
broadcaster"); (b) disperse that bundle to `k−1 = 2` independent third-party
replicas *and confirm they hold it* before broadcasting; (c) pay the ~$0.58
on-chain inscription fee from a Bitcoin UTXO (which **exposes a funding UTXO** —
the very metadata leak the broadcaster-paid model exists to avoid); and (d)
**serve the bundle indefinitely** as the long-term holder (§4.6), or trust that
scanners will.

For a **phone wallet** this is not a realistic fallback: it lacks the proving
horsepower for a recursive aggregate proof at acceptable latency/battery, lacks
an always-on relay to serve the bundle, and lacks a spendable Bitcoin UTXO that
it is willing to doxx. **"Permissionless" is true in the formal sense (no
gatekeeper) but degrades in practice to "pay any *willing* publisher."** If
every willing publisher censors a spender (or the only willing publishers form a
cartel), the phone-wallet user's real fallback is "find a different willing
publisher," not "self-publish." This is a meaningful weakening of the
censorship-resilience claim. **Censorship-resilience verdict: STRONG against a
single censoring publisher (others exist), WEAK against a censoring publisher
*cartel* for low-capability (phone) wallets, because the self-publish escape
hatch is economically and operationally out of reach for exactly the clients
that most need it.**

### 2. Sequential `prev_root` monopoly — the structural MEV surface

This is the single most important liveness/incentive finding and deserves its
own treatment.

**The mechanism.** §3.4 / §3.6 step 4: each `BatchInscription` must declare
`prev_root` = the most recently admitted `new_root`, and **only one batch lands
per accumulator tip**, ordered by Bitcoin canonical position. Two publishers
racing the same `prev_root` is "normal"; the later one is rejected as stale and
its `AggregateBatchProof` work is **wasted**.

This is a **winner-take-all serialized write lock on global state, arbitrated by
the Bitcoin fee market.** That has three rational-actor consequences the spec
does not price:

**(a) Chain-bid monopoly (R-D4-2).** A well-funded publisher can simply outbid
(fee-snipe) every tip slot — broadcast its inscription at a higher fee rate than
any rival for the *next* `prev_root`, repeatedly. It breaks **no rule**: it
declares the correct `prev_root`, its proof verifies, it wins block position by
paying more. The result is a **de-facto exclusive writer**. The on-chain
metadata (publisher identity `Pkₚ` is in every inscription, §3.5) then
concentrates: one entity sees/orders the entire global spend flow. This is the
classic MEV analogue — *ordering and inclusion control = metadata extraction +
censorship power* — achieved without any consensus-level privilege.

**(b) Selfish-mining-analogue stale-forcing / griefing (R-D4-3).** A malicious
or merely competitive publisher who lands first **repeatedly** forces every
rival's in-flight batch stale (§3.6 step 4), **destroying the rivals' proving
work** (~100 KB recursive proofs, real CPU). The attacker's cost is one extra
on-chain fee per slot (~$0.58 + the fee premium to win). The victim's cost is
the full proving cost of a wasted `AggregateBatchProof`.

> **Griefing ratio.** Attacker marginal cost to force a rival stale ≈ one
> inscription + fee-bid premium (order $0.6–few-$). Victim cost ≈ one wasted
> recursive aggregate-proof build (CPU-seconds to minutes of proving, the
> dominant cost in the whole system per §3.8: "the cost equation has shifted …
> to per-batch publisher proving cost"). Because the attacker batches its
> *own* real records into the winning inscription, its proving work is **not
> wasted** — it would have done it anyway. So the *net* griefing cost to the
> attacker is just the **fee premium**, while the victim eats a **whole wasted
> proof**. The ratio is favourable to the attacker (griefing cost << damage
> inflicted), and worse still, the attacker is simultaneously *earning* fees
> from its own batched records. **This is a profitable strategy, not mere
> griefing**, for any publisher with enough capital and throughput to win the
> slot consistently.

**(c) Throughput ceiling interaction.** Because admissions are strictly
serialized (one batch per tip, chained `prev_root`), total system throughput is
bounded by *how fast batches can be confirmed and chained*, and the §3.4
RECOMMENDED 6-block stale-retry window means a sniped publisher waits ~1 hour
before its re-batched work can even retry. Under contention, the rational
equilibrium is **professionalization**: only well-capitalized, low-latency,
high-fee publishers survive the slot race; small/altruistic publishers are
priced/sniped out. The privacy surface (publisher sees the flow) and the
censorship surface (publisher controls inclusion) therefore **concentrate onto
the same few professional publishers** — which is precisely the "no central
element anywhere" principle eroding under economic pressure rather than under
attack.

**The spec's stated mitigation is incomplete.** §3.4's stale-bundle handling
(re-batch within 6 blocks, or release records) prevents *loss* of records but
does nothing about the *wasted-work / monopoly* dynamic — it just tells the
loser to try again (and pay again, and re-prove). There is no anti-MEV ordering
mechanism (no commit-reveal slot auction, no fair-ordering, no proof-of-burn
ticketing), because there cannot be one without consensus — and zkCoins
deliberately adds no consensus. **This is an inherent, unpriced cost of the
"only Bitcoin, no consensus" design choice.**

### 3. Relay / store operator (data-availability provider)

**Honest strategy & cost.** Every node is a full Nostr relay (§4.1). It MUST
retain delivery events + blobs (§4.2 store-and-forward) and, as a scanner, MUST
retain every admitted `BatchBundle` **indefinitely** and SHOULD serve it on
request (§4.6 long-term-retention). Storage grows monotonically and **cannot be
pruned by age** (§3.7: nullifiers are uniformly distributed keys, "old" maps to
nothing discardable). Bandwidth to serve bundles to every recovering/scanning
peer is ongoing.

**Revenue.** **There is none in the protocol.** §4.6 supplies a long-term
holder for `BatchBundle`s by *fiat* ("this rule supplies one: every node …
MUST retain"), but attaches **no fee, no reward, no payment** for storage or
serving. The spec's economics (§3.8) price only the publisher's inscription and
proving; storage and serving are simply **assumed to be borne** by node
operators out of self-interest (running a node to serve *your own* wallet) or
altruism (archival services).

**Deviation strategies & the tragedy of the commons (R-D4-4).**

- A rational operator serves bundles **its own users need** and has zero
  incentive to serve the **long tail** — bundles for accounts it does not host,
  especially *old* batches whose recovery demand is rare. The §4.6
  prune-guard ("verify ≥3 peers still hold it before pruning") is **MUST**, but
  there is no audit, no slashing, and no reward for honesty: a rational
  storage-pressured operator simply prunes and **claims** it checked, or prunes
  bundles it judges no one will ask for. Detection is impossible until someone
  actually needs the bundle — *years later, during recovery*.
- The §4.6 claim that "the practical replica count grows monotonically with the
  scanning-node network" assumes scanners are **numerous, honest, and
  permanent**. Each of those is an *incentive* assumption, not a guarantee.
  Under cost pressure the equilibrium is **under-provision of long-tail DA**:
  every operator rationally hopes *some other* operator keeps the old bundle.
  This is the textbook tragedy of the commons.

**Connection to P9's reality.** P9's MEDIUM-liveness grade is *exactly* this:
recovery's false-reject mode "is liveness, not safety … the only failure mode is
unavailability." The P9 certificate abstracts replica reachability to a
non-deterministic candidate set `Q` (the strongest adversary) and explicitly
declines to model "the *quantitative* durability that `k=3` buys" or "operator
independence" — those are "the liveness half, which is MEDIUM and out of the
unbounded certificate." **In plain terms: the formal proof guarantees you won't
be tricked into accepting a forged bundle on recovery; it guarantees nothing
about whether your genuine bundle still exists to be recovered.** And the actor
analysis here shows the genuine bundle's *existence* depends on an
**uncompensated** storage commons — i.e. the liveness assumption P9 brackets out
is, under rational play, **the most likely thing to fail at long time horizons.**
A coin whose every `CoinProof` replica is dropped becomes **permanently
unspendable** (§4.6: "If every holder drops it … the coin becomes
unspendable") — and the protocol pays no one to prevent that.

### 4. Spender

**Honest strategy & cost.** Build the per-account recursive proof, sign the
`SpendRecord`, hand it to a publisher with a fee allowance, deliver the
`CoinProof` to the receiver, retain the bundle + `K_tx` until ACK + `k`
confirmed (§4.2/§4.6), and **self-deliver** the change/account-state bundle to
its own relays (§4.2 self-delivery — "without it neither [multi-device sync nor
recovery] works").

**Deviations.** A spender's adversarial options are mostly *safety*-bounded
(can't double-spend, nullifier prevents replay — threat-model S4). The
incentive-relevant deviations are:

- **D4.1 Free-ride on self-delivery DA** — skip replicating its own
  change/state bundle to `k` independent holders to save bandwidth. *Self-harm,
  not an attack*: it risks losing its own change coin / breaking its own
  recovery. Rational spenders comply *for their own benefit* — this is one of
  the few roles where incentives genuinely align with the spec.
- **D4.2 Fee front-running** (threat-model S5) — outbid in the mempool to win
  inclusion position. Endemic Bitcoin MEV; for the core accumulator this folds
  into the §2 sequential-prev_root race (the spender's *publisher* is the one
  bidding). Accepted residual.

**Verdict for spenders: incentives largely align** — the self-interest of
getting your own coins delivered/recovered enforces spec-conformance for the
self-delivery and ACK rules. This is the protocol's strongest incentive story.

### 5. Receiver

**Honest strategy & cost.** Scan candidate delivery events, compute one
Poseidon hash per candidate to match `detect_tag` (§4.4), trial-decrypt
matches, verify against Bitcoin (§4.5), and **return a signed ACK** (§4.2).

**Deviation: never ACK (R-D4-5).** The §4.2 ACK rule is **asymmetric**: the
*sender* MUST retain its copy and re-publish on exponential backoff (30 s → 1 h
cap) **until a valid ACK arrives**, and MUST NOT drop the copy before ACK + `k`.
A receiver that simply **never ACKs** — whether maliciously, or because its node
is offline, or out of laziness (it already got the coin, why spend bandwidth
ACKing?) — imposes an **unbounded retention + retry obligation on the sender**.
The spec sets no ACK deadline and no sender-side give-up condition; literally
read, the sender retains and retries **forever**.

> *Profitable?* No direct gain to the receiver — pure griefing / negligence.
> But the cost is **real and one-sided**: every never-ACKed delivery is a
> permanent storage + periodic-bandwidth tax on the sender. At scale, a
> receiver population that is merely *lazy* (ACKing is uncompensated work)
> degrades every sender's resource budget. There is **no incentive to ACK** —
> the receiver has already received and verified the coin before the ACK is due
> (§4.2: ACK is sent "on successful receipt and verification"). The ACK is
> pure altruism toward the sender. **Rational receivers under-ACK.**

- **D5.2 ACK-then-claim-loss** social attack — ACK, then later claim "I never
  got it" to pressure a re-send or a refund out-of-band. The signed ACK
  (`op` signature over `ack_message` binding `detect_tag‖blob_id‖ack_nonce`) is
  the sender's **cryptographic receipt** and defeats this at the protocol level
  — a strong point. Residual is purely social/legal, off-protocol.

### 6. Prover-node economics & the fee market (R-D4-6)

**The core question:** is there a stable fee market for proving, or a race to
centralization? The cost structure (§3.8): per-batch on-chain cost is constant
and *trivial* (~$0.58); the dominant cost is **publisher proving + bundle DA
bandwidth**. Revenue is the **MAY-compensation**, contingent (per the source
paper) on **winning the inclusion race** (§2).

Combine these and the equilibrium is **adverse**:

- Proving a recursive aggregate is heavy and has economies of scale (amortize
  fixed proving infra, bigger batches amortize the constant on-chain cost
  harder — §3.4 "larger batches amortise … more aggressively"). → favours
  **few large professional publishers**.
- Fee revenue requires winning the serialized slot race (§2). → favours
  **well-capitalized publishers who can fee-snipe**.
- A publisher who builds a proof but loses the slot earns **nothing** (the fee
  predicate only pays the first to publish). → small/occasional publishers face
  **negative expected value** under contention and exit.

The rational equilibrium is therefore **professionalized centralization of
publishing**: a small number of professional publishers who (a) see the entire
spend flow (privacy surface), (b) control inclusion/ordering (censorship
surface), and (c) are the only entities for whom self-publish is not a fallback
but a *business*. The spec's claim "no publisher can extract rent because
publishing is permissionless" (§3.8) is **formally true but economically
fragile**: permissionless *entry* does not prevent *oligopoly* when fixed costs,
slot-race capital requirements, and winner-take-all fee payment all push the
same way. **There is no designed fee market** (no auction, no reservation, no
reward smoothing) — fees are bilateral and opaque, which is good for privacy but
gives no price-discovery or anti-concentration pressure.

### 7. Asset creator

**Honest strategy.** Mint via `InitialProof`; permissionless asset creation
(spec principle). Mints may be unanchored (`mint-verified`, §3.10).

**Deviations** are mostly *application/social*, not core-protocol-liveness:
asset-issuance spam / name-squatting (threat-model XA3 — residual: UX, asset_id
is a hash, no namespace conflict), pump-and-dump (E1 — standard crypto risk).
There is one **DA-relevant** note: an asset creator who mints and then never
provisions DA for the mint bundle leaves holders unable to *spend* later (a
spend needs the prior bundle). This collapses into the §3 storage-commons
problem. **No new core-liveness exposure beyond DA under-provision.**

### 8. Bitcoin miner

**Honest strategy.** Include zkCoins reveal transactions like any other,
ordered by fee.

**Deviation: censor `0x42 0x42`-marked envelopes (R-D4-8).** The marker prefix
`0x42 0x42` ("BB") is in the **clear** at a fixed offset in every reveal witness
(§3.5), expressly so scanners can cheaply skip non-zkCoins inscriptions. The
same visibility lets a **regulator-pressured or self-censoring miner** identify
and **drop all zkCoins inscriptions** trivially.

- *Single miner:* threat-model M1 — target delayed one block, another miner
  includes it. Minor.
- *Coalition / regulatory scenario:* if a *majority* of hashrate refuses to mine
  `0x42 0x42` envelopes (a plausible regulatory-pressure outcome for a privacy
  protocol), **the entire accumulator stalls** — no batch can be admitted,
  global liveness halts. This is **not** a per-user UX delay; it is a
  protocol-level liveness kill switch handed to Bitcoin miners by the
  *cleartext marker*.
- *Reordering (M2):* the §2 sequential-prev_root race already gives miners
  ordering power over which racing batch wins — a bribed miner can pick the
  winner of a tip-slot race, monetizing the §2 MEV surface.

> **Marker-visibility tradeoff.** The 231-byte inscription has a strong on-chain
> anonymity story for *contents* (record count hidden, amounts/parties/graph
> invisible — §3.5 metadata note). But the **fact that a transaction is
> zkCoins** is *maximally* visible (a fixed 2-byte cleartext flag). For
> censorship-resistance against miners/regulators this is the **worst** choice:
> the protocol is trivially fingerprintable at L1. The anonymity set protects
> *who/what/how-much* but not *that-it-happened-and-it's-zkCoins*. A
> regulator does not need to break privacy to suppress the protocol — it only
> needs to tell miners to drop `0x4242`. *Profitable for the miner?* Only under
> external (regulatory) pressure or bribe; not spontaneously profitable. But the
> **capability** is unconditionally present and cheap, and the design makes it
> *cheaper* than it needs to be.

## Equilibrium assessment

**Does the network converge to spec-conformant behaviour?** Role by role:

- **Spenders: YES.** Self-interest (deliver/recover my own coins) enforces the
  self-delivery, retention, and replication rules. The strongest incentive
  story in the protocol.
- **Receivers: NO (under-ACK).** ACKing is uncompensated altruism performed
  *after* the receiver already has the coin. Rational receivers under-ACK,
  imposing unbounded retention/retry on senders. Mitigated only by client
  software defaults and the fact that most nodes auto-ACK — i.e. by *convention*,
  not incentive.
- **Publishers / prover-nodes: NO (professionalized centralization).** Heavy
  recursive proving with economies of scale + winner-take-all fee payment +
  serialized slot race ⇒ a small oligopoly of well-capitalized professional
  publishers. Permissionless *entry* does not prevent *concentration*. The
  privacy and censorship surfaces concentrate onto the same few entities — the
  "no central element" principle erodes under economics, not under attack.
- **Relays / storage operators: NO (under-provision of long-tail DA).**
  Indefinite, unprunable, **uncompensated** storage is a textbook commons.
  Operators rationally serve their own users and hope someone else keeps old
  bundles. This is precisely the liveness failure mode P9 grades MEDIUM and
  brackets out of its certificate.
- **Bitcoin miners: spec-conformant absent external pressure**, but the
  cleartext `0x4242` marker hands them a cheap, unconditional censorship
  capability that becomes a **global liveness kill switch** under regulatory
  pressure or majority collusion.

**Overall equilibrium prediction: a hybrid of (b) professionalized
centralization (publishing) and (c) under-provision (long-tail DA), not (a)
spec-conformant decentralization.** The protocol is **safe** (P1–P10 hold) in
all of these equilibria — no theft, no forgery. But its *stated political
properties* ("no central element anywhere," "permissionless," "censorship
resistant for a phone wallet") are **incentive-fragile**: they hold against
*single* adversaries and under *altruistic* operation, and degrade under
*rational, cost-minimizing, profit-maximizing* play.

**Final honest answer to "are you sure rational actors play spec-conform?"**

**No.** Specifically:

1. **Receivers** rationally **under-ACK** (uncompensated post-receipt altruism)
   → unbounded sender retention obligation.
2. **Publishers** rationally **fee-snipe the serialized tip slot** → de-facto
   monopoly writer with full ordering/metadata/censorship control, and
   profitably grief rivals' proving work (griefing ratio favours the attacker).
3. **Storage/relay operators** rationally **under-provide long-tail DA**
   (uncompensated, unprunable, monotonic storage commons) → the exact MEDIUM
   liveness gap P9 declines to certify; coins/recovery can fail at long horizons.
4. **Prover-nodes** rationally **consolidate** into a professional oligopoly
   under economies-of-scale + winner-take-all fees → centralization by economics.

Spenders are the bright spot (incentives align). For everyone else, the spec
relies on **altruism, convention, or self-interest-in-one's-own-data** rather
than designed incentive compatibility — which is acceptable for *safety* but
exactly the wrong foundation for *liveness*. The verification base agrees:
**nothing in P1–P10 guarantees progress, and P9 explicitly bracket-outs the one
liveness property as MEDIUM, contingent on assumptions this analysis shows
rational actors do not reliably uphold.**

---

*Bottom line: the protocol is engineered for **safety without trusted parties**
and largely achieves it. **Liveness, however, is engineered as an assumption,
not a mechanism** — it is left to altruism (storage/serving), convention (ACK),
and a fee model that, under rational play, concentrates publishing into a
professional oligopoly while leaving long-tail data availability systematically
under-provided. Rational actors do **not** uniformly play spec-conform; they
play safe-but-self-interested, and the gap between those two is exactly the
liveness risk surface.*
