# D5 — Comparison With Related Designs

> Concept-viability review, dimension D5. What zkCoins *is* relative to the prior
> art, what it does differently, which failure modes of those systems also threaten
> it, and which risks are zkCoins-specific. Honest and tabular; no marketing.

## Methodology

The baseline for "what zkCoins is" is the post-`docs#40` specification
([`specification.md`](../../../docs/docs/specification.md)) and the verified-property
scope in [`../CERTIFICATE.md`](../CERTIFICATE.md)
(P1–P10 verified against axiomatized primitives A1–A17 and meta-assumptions M1–M4;
the **reference implementation is explicitly out of scope** — F15 is the deployment
gate). The closest relative, **Shielded CSV** (Nick–Eagen–Linus, ePrint 2025/068, the
construction zkCoins realizes), is read directly from
[`shieldedcsv-paper.pdf`](../../shieldedcsv-paper.pdf); its own §6 (Discussion) and §1.1
(Contributions) supply its acknowledged assumptions and limitations. The project's
self-positioning in [`notes/l2-protocol-design.md`](../../notes/l2-protocol-design.md)
and [`notes/threat-model.md`](../../notes/threat-model.md) describes an **older SSS-based
L2 variant that predates `docs#40`**; where it conflicts with the current spec (e.g. an
SSS/bond/soft-finality layer that no longer exists), the spec and certificate govern,
and the divergence is noted rather than carried.

For each comparison system the **known failure modes** are drawn from the public,
documented record (cited inline), not from marketing. A failure mode is mapped onto
zkCoins only where a concrete mechanism in the spec makes it applicable. Finding IDs
`R-D5-n` carry a severity (impact if it materializes) and likelihood, consistent with
the D1–D4 convention.

The comparison set is: **Shielded CSV** (closest base), **Zcash** (shielded pool),
**Monero** (ring signatures), **Tornado Cash** (mixer), **Aztec / Aztec Connect**
(privacy zk-rollup), and **Lightning** (off-chain Bitcoin). RGB / Taproot Assets are
referenced as the CSV ancestors that frame the data-availability problem.

## What zkCoins is (honest one-paragraph)

zkCoins is **one concrete, opinionated instantiation of Shielded CSV on Bitcoin**: a
client-side-validation protocol in which the chain stores only opaque markers, coins
travel off-chain as encrypted bearer bundles carrying a constant-size recursive (PCD)
validity proof, and double-spend protection is a global nullifier accumulator whose
state transitions are anchored on Bitcoin L1. It adds, on top of the paper, four things
the paper left open or out of scope: (1) it relocates the per-batch data **off-chain**
into a content-addressed `BatchBundle` and writes only a constant **231-byte
`BatchInscription`** (`prev_root → new_root` + publisher key + locator + signature) per
batch, so the on-chain footprint is constant in batch size rather than the paper's
"64 bytes per transaction"; (2) it specifies a concrete **delivery and recovery layer**
(Nostr NIP-44/59 transport, seed-derivable detection/scan keys, `k = 3` replication)
that the paper explicitly defers ("further research … future work"); (3) it is
**multi-asset** with permissionless, creator-bound issuance; and (4) it pins a concrete,
**trusted-setup-free** proof instantiation (Plonky2-style FRI recursion over Goldilocks +
Poseidon). It has **no own chain, token, consensus change, or trusted operator**, and
its safety properties (no forgery, no double-spend, conservation, ZK, client-side
validation) are mechanically verified at the spec level — but only the **specification**;
the proving circuits, the off-chain DA, and the liveness/incentive layer are where the
residual risk concentrates (see D3, D4, and below).

## Per-system: shared failure modes that threaten zkCoins

### Shielded CSV (the closest base) — zkCoins inherits its hardest problem

**What it is / model.** Shielded CSV is the construction zkCoins realizes: PCD coin
proofs, a nullifier accumulator, permissionless publishers, constant-size coin proofs,
no trusted setup. **What zkCoins does differently:** it moves the per-batch data
off-chain (constant `BatchInscription` instead of one on-chain nullifier per tx), pins a
concrete proof system and key hierarchy, adds multi-asset issuance, and **specifies the
delivery/recovery layer the paper deferred**.

**Shared failure mode that threatens zkCoins (the inherited hard problem):**
the **data-availability / bearer-recovery problem of all CSV systems**. The paper is
explicit: coin proofs travel off-chain over "communication channels," the static
donation-address delivery case is "future work" (§6.1), and "if the wallet state is lost,
the account balance becomes irretrievable" (§6.2). This is the same structural problem
RGB has — there is **no on-chain source of truth to re-sync from**, so losing local
proofs renders assets unprovable ([6](https://www.nervos.org/knowledge-base/ultimate_guide_to_rgb_rgbpp_and_client_side_validation)).
zkCoins *mitigates* this more than the paper does (seed-derivable detection/scan keys so
incoming coins can be re-discovered; `k = 3` replication of bearer bundles; a concrete
Nostr mesh) — but it does **not escape it**: recovery still depends on off-chain bundles
remaining available, and D3/D4 already grade long-tail DA as the protocol's MEDIUM
liveness gap. **`R-D5-1`.**

**zkCoins additionally *worsens* one DA axis relative to the paper.** In the paper, the
double-spend-relevant datum (the nullifier) is **on Bitcoin**; only the *coin proof* is
off-chain, so a DA loss harms one coin's recipient. In zkCoins, the nullifiers and their
`AggregateBatchProof` live **off-chain in the `BatchBundle`**, and a scanner cannot
advance the accumulator past a `BatchInscription` whose bundle it cannot fetch (spec §3.6
step 5 leaves it `pending`; §3.10). So one unavailable bundle can **gate accumulator
progress and receive-side verification for everyone**, not just one coin — a strictly
larger blast radius than the paper's design. This is zkCoins-specific in *severity* and is
revisited under specific failure modes. **`R-D5-2`.**

**What zkCoins avoids vs the bare paper.** It avoids the paper's unspecified
bridge-to-Bitcoin-value problem only by *not bridging* (it is an issuance/asset layer, not
wrapped BTC); it avoids the per-transaction on-chain byte cost (constant per batch); and
it gives the deferred recovery story a concrete, partial answer.

### Zcash — the soundness-bug and global-shielded-pool lessons

**What it is / model.** Own-chain shielded pool; ZK notes + nullifiers; Orchard is
trusted-setup-free (Halo2). Settlement is Zcash's own PoW consensus.
**What zkCoins does differently:** no own chain (Bitcoin L1 only); notes/proofs off-chain
rather than in blocks; recursion (PCD) instead of per-tx SNARK; trusted-setup-free like
Orchard.

**Shared failure modes that threaten zkCoins:**
- **ZK-circuit soundness bug → counterfeiting.** The June 2026 Orchard incident was a
  soundness bug in the proof *circuit implementation* (`halo2_gadgets`), undetected for
  ~4 years, that could have allowed in-pool forgery/double-spend
  ([1](https://www.blockhead.co/2026/06/05/zcash-founder-discloses-critical-orchard-forgery-flaw-fixed-by-emergency-hard-fork/),
  [7](https://beincrypto.com/zcash-zec-orchard-counterfeiting-bug/)). zkCoins's safety
  (P1 no-forgery, P3 conservation) is **verified only at the spec level**, with the
  primitives axiomatized (A1–A17) and the **reference implementation explicitly out of
  scope** (F15). The Orchard bug is precisely an F15-class failure: spec-sound, circuit
  unsound. zkCoins's recursion makes the trusted computing base **larger** (a bug in the
  cyclic verifier or `C_batch` aggregation propagates to every coin), and there is **no
  turnstile-equivalent supply firewall** — Zcash's turnstile capped the blast radius of
  the bug to in-pool double-spend rather than unbounded supply inflation; zkCoins has
  per-asset conservation in-circuit but **no independent, non-ZK accounting backstop** if
  that very circuit is unsound. **`R-D5-3` (the single most consequential shared risk).**
- **Privacy hides the breach.** As with Zcash, zkCoins's privacy means an exploited
  forgery is **not externally auditable** — the same property that protects users prevents
  detection of inflation. (Severity multiplier on R-D5-3, not a separate finding.)
- **Optional-privacy / low-anonymity dilution.** Zcash's shielded set was historically
  weak because most value sat transparent. zkCoins is **shielded-only** (no transparent
  mode), so it avoids *this specific* dilution — see "What zkCoins genuinely improves on."

### Monero — anonymity-set and graph-analysis lessons

**What it is / model.** Own-chain; ring signatures (bounded ring size 16) + stealth
addresses + RingCT. **What zkCoins does differently:** anonymity is a **global PCD set**,
not a bounded per-input ring; there are no decoys to mis-sample; the transaction graph is
not on-chain at all.

**Shared failure modes that threaten zkCoins:**
- **Off-chain / network-graph correlation (the EAE analogue).** Monero's residual
  leakage is *graph* leakage — repeated interaction with a tracing counterparty, decoy
  mismatch, flooding ([2](https://www.getmonero.org/2025/04/05/ospead-optimal-ring-signature-research.html),
  [8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05332)). zkCoins removes the *on-chain* graph, but the
  Shielded CSV paper itself warns (§6.1) that an adversary intercepting the delivery
  channels can reconstruct the graph, and (§6.3) that **coins minted in one transaction
  are linkable by their recipients** — a colluding-recipient deanonymization identical in
  spirit to EAE. zkCoins's Nostr transport (NIP-59 gift-wrap) and per-coin fresh `epk`
  address the metadata layer, but the **CERTIFICATE grades P5 on-chain HIGH / network
  MEDIUM** — the network-layer privacy is explicitly *not* certified, exactly where Monero
  bleeds. **`R-D5-4`.**
- **Detect-tag scan as a side channel.** Monero scanning is per-output trial; zkCoins
  uses a per-event Poseidon `detect_tag`. It does not reduce the *count* of candidates a
  recipient must pull (spec §1.3), so a recipient's pull pattern over the relay mesh is a
  metadata surface analogous to Monero's chain-scan observability. (Folded into R-D5-4.)

### Tornado Cash — anonymity-set economics and compliance

**What it is / model.** Fixed-denomination Ethereum mixer; per-pool anonymity set; trusted
per-pool setup. **What zkCoins does differently:** one global set (no per-denomination
fragmentation), no fixed denominations (arbitrary amounts hidden in-circuit), no per-pool
ceremony.

**Shared failure modes that threaten zkCoins:**
- **"Poisoned / unrefusable inbound" compliance taint.** Anyone could send Tornado-tainted
  dust to a victim who could not refuse it, creating compliance exposure
  ([3](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/tornado-cash-sanctions-challenges/)). zkCoins
  coins are likewise **bearer and unrefusable at the protocol level** — the threat-model's
  S9 dust-attack and the "no diversified addresses; the account is the sole isolation unit"
  rule (spec §1.2) mean a recipient cannot cryptographically reject inbound coins to their
  one address. This is a real shared exposure. **`R-D5-5`.**
- **Protocol-level censorship / sanctions surface.** Tornado showed validators/miners will
  censor an identifiable privacy protocol under legal pressure. zkCoins's **cleartext
  `0x4242` inscription marker** is a trivial L1 fingerprint (already flagged R-D4-8): it
  hands miners the same blanket-censorship capability OFAC pressure produced on Ethereum.
  (Cross-reference to R-D4-8; counted there, reinforced here.)

**What zkCoins avoids.** It avoids per-pool anonymity-set fragmentation and the
liquidity-drain collapse that entrenched Tornado's decline — its set is global and does
not require fresh deposits to stay large.

### Aztec Connect — the centralized-sequencer / single-writer lesson

**What it is / model.** Ethereum privacy zk-rollup with on-chain DA but a **single
sequencer**; trusted PLONK setup. It was sunset in 2023 because it could not be
decentralized without a rewrite, leaving users dependent on the operator (or a forked
sequencer) to exit ([4](https://medium.com/aztec-protocol/sunsetting-aztec-connect-a786edce5cae),
[9](https://www.theblock.co/post/219253/aztec-to-sunset-private-zk-rollup-connect-and-focus-on-next-gen-layer-2-chain)).

**Shared failure mode that threatens zkCoins:**
- **De-facto single-writer centralization.** Aztec's killer was a single sequencer.
  zkCoins is *permissionless* in principle, but D4 (R-D4-2) shows the **single sequential
  `prev_root` slot** plus winner-take-all fees plus heavy recursive proving drive the
  publisher role toward a professional **oligopoly / de-facto single writer** — the same
  centralization Aztec could not escape, arrived at by economics rather than by design.
  Unlike Aztec, zkCoins users are **not** custodially trapped (no operator holds funds;
  self-publish exists for capable nodes), so the *exit* story is better — but the
  *liveness and metadata-concentration* story rhymes exactly with Aztec's. (Cross-reference
  R-D4-2; reinforced, not double-counted.)

**What zkCoins avoids.** No trusted setup (Aztec needed an SRS); no custodial operator who
must stay alive for users to withdraw (zkCoins coins are bearer, spendable via any
publisher).

### Lightning — off-chain liveness and the operator-holds-keys lesson

**What it is / model.** Bitcoin payment channels; off-chain state held by both parties;
watchtowers for safety while offline; no ledger privacy. **What zkCoins does differently:**
it is a shielded *ledger* with a global accumulator, not bilateral channels; there is no
penalty-transaction race and no requirement that the *recipient* be online to stay safe.

**Shared failure modes that threaten zkCoins:**
- **Off-chain-state liveness dependence.** Lightning users lose safety/liquidity if their
  state is lost or they are offline without a watchtower; griefing/jamming freezes
  liquidity ([5](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/technical/good-griefing-a-lingering-vulnerability-on-lightning-network-that-still-needs-fixing),
  [10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09327)). zkCoins likewise depends on **off-chain state
  that the chain cannot reconstruct** (the bearer bundles) and on a **node holding the
  operational `{ivk, ovk, op}` bundle** to receive 24/7 on the wallet's behalf (spec §1.2).
  The dependence is weaker than Lightning's (no time-critical penalty window, no per-channel
  liquidity), but the **"your funds depend on off-chain data + a node being available and
  honest about availability"** shape is shared and is the D3 operator-trust surface.
  (Cross-reference D3; reinforced.)
- **Operator holds the full view bundle.** A zkCoins node entrusted with `{ivk, ovk, op}`
  sees the user's entire incoming and outgoing graph for that account — it cannot spend
  (no SPEND branch), but it is a complete **privacy** honeypot, analogous to a Lightning
  routing/LSP node's view of a user's payments. This is the D3 finding restated; it is
  zkCoins-relevant but not Lightning-unique. (Cross-reference D3.)

## zkCoins-specific failure modes (none of the comparison systems have all of these)

These arise from zkCoins's particular `docs#40` architecture and have no clean analogue in
the systems above.

1. **Off-chain `BatchBundle` DA gates global accumulator progress (R-D5-2, restated as the
   headline specific risk).** Unlike every on-chain-DA system (Zcash, Monero, Tornado,
   Aztec) and unlike the paper (nullifier on-chain), zkCoins keeps the
   double-spend-relevant data — nullifiers + `AggregateBatchProof` — **off-chain**. A single
   unreachable bundle leaves its `BatchInscription` `pending` and **blocks every honest
   scanner from advancing the accumulator past that point** (spec §3.6 step 5, §3.10), which
   stalls *all* receive-side non-membership checks downstream, not just one coin. No
   comparison system has the property that one missing off-chain blob can freeze the global
   double-spend ledger for everyone. **Severity HIGH; likelihood Medium** (a rational,
   uncompensated DA commons — see D4 R-D4-7 — makes long-tail bundle loss expected).

2. **Single sequential global accumulator writer (the `prev_root` monopoly).** The
   accumulator is a **strictly serial** structure: each `BatchInscription` must chain
   `prev_root = live tip`, so at most one publisher's batch lands per accumulator slot and
   racing batches are wasted (spec §3.4, §3.6 step 4). This is structurally unlike Monero's
   parallel key-images, Zcash's per-block nullifier set, or Tornado's independent notes. It
   creates a **winner-take-all writer race** with no consensus to arbitrate it — the D4
   centralization driver (R-D4-2/3) — and is the mechanism by which zkCoins reproduces
   Aztec's single-writer outcome without choosing it. **Severity HIGH; likelihood High**
   (emerges under any contention). (Primary finding owned by D4; listed here as the
   *comparative* differentiator.)

3. **Operator holds `ivk + ovk + op` simultaneously (full-graph view honeypot).** To
   receive 24/7 a wallet entrusts a node with the entire operational view bundle (spec §1.2).
   No single party in Zcash/Monero (own keys), Tornado (no accounts), or Lightning (sees only
   routed amounts, not a shielded ledger) holds an equivalent *complete incoming+outgoing*
   view of a user's private ledger while being a third party. **Severity MEDIUM (privacy,
   not custody); likelihood High** (the default deployment for any always-on wallet). Owned
   by D3; the *comparative* point is that this concentration is sharper than in any peer
   system. **`R-D5-6` covers the recovery half (below); the view-honeypot half is D3.**

4. **Seed alone does not recover spendable balance (R-D5-1 made specific as R-D5-6).**
   Bitcoin, Zcash, Monero, and Tornado all recover spend authority from seed/secret +
   public chain. zkCoins (like Lightning and RGB) **cannot**: the seed re-derives only the
   *scan/detection* keys (`ivk`, `dk`), letting a wallet *re-discover* incoming coins, but
   **the unspent `CoinProof` bundles must still be fetched from the relay mesh**, and a
   coin's spendability requires its bundle to be available. The certificate grades P9 as
   "correctness unbounded / **liveness MEDIUM**" precisely here. This is a sharper recovery
   gap than any non-CSV peer and is the user-facing face of R-D5-1. **Severity HIGH;
   likelihood Medium** (depends on DA over time; same root as R-D5-2). **`R-D5-6`.**

5. **Detect-tag scan bandwidth (no candidate-count reduction).** The deterministic
   `detect_tag` scheme is cheap per event but **does not reduce how many candidate events a
   recipient must download** (spec §1.3); optional fuzzy message detection is the only lever
   and is not specified as mandatory. As the global event stream grows, every recipient's
   scan bandwidth grows with **total network activity**, not with their own activity — a
   scaling/liveness pressure (see D7) absent from systems where you scan only your own
   chain history (Monero/Zcash full nodes still scan all, but light-client filtering exists;
   zkCoins's mesh-pull model concentrates the cost on the recipient's bundle fetch).
   **Severity MEDIUM; likelihood High** at scale. **`R-D5-7`.**

## What zkCoins genuinely improves on

Honest credits, not marketing:

- **vs Zcash/Monero (privacy coins):** no own chain, no own token, no own consensus — it
  inherits Bitcoin's settlement rather than bootstrapping new security; and it is
  **shielded-only**, so it does not suffer Zcash's transparent-pool dilution of the
  anonymity set.
- **vs Tornado Cash:** one **global** anonymity set with arbitrary amounts, instead of
  fragmented fixed-denomination pools that collapse as liquidity drains; **no trusted
  per-pool ceremony**.
- **vs Aztec Connect:** **no trusted setup** and **no custodial operator** — users hold
  bearer coins spendable via any publisher, so there is no "the operator must keep the
  sequencer alive or you cannot exit" trap that sunset Aztec Connect.
- **vs Lightning:** no per-channel inbound-liquidity management, no time-critical
  watchtower penalty window, and the **recipient need not be online to stay safe** (only to
  *receive*); it is a shielded ledger, not bilateral channels.
- **vs RGB / Taproot Assets (CSV ancestors):** **constant-size** coin proofs (PCD recursion)
  instead of proofs that grow with transaction history, and it **hides the transaction
  history from the recipient**, which RGB/Taproot Assets do not.
- **vs the bare Shielded CSV paper:** a concrete, partial answer to the recovery/delivery
  layer the paper deferred (seed-derivable scan keys + Nostr + `k=3`), constant per-batch
  on-chain cost, and multi-asset issuance.

The throughline: zkCoins's genuine wins are all on the **"no central element / no new trust
base"** axis. Its genuine *risks* are the mirror image — by refusing a consensus layer it
inherits **CSV's data-availability problem** and adds a **serial single-writer + off-chain
DA-gates-everyone** twist that is sharper than the paper's, and its safety rests on an
**unverified circuit implementation** (the exact class of bug that hit Zcash Orchard).

| **R-D5-6** | Make the **"no recovery from seed alone"** property explicit and user-facing (it differs from Bitcoin/Zcash/Monero expectations). Specify the maximum survivable DA-replica loss and the wallet UX for "coin discovered but bundle unavailable." | HIGH | Medium | Users will assume seed-only recovery; the truth (P9 liveness MEDIUM) must be surfaced |
| **R-D5-7** | Quantify **recipient scan bandwidth** growth with total network activity and elevate fuzzy message detection from OPTIONAL toward RECOMMENDED at scale; coordinate with D7. | MEDIUM | High (at scale) | Detect-tag does not cut candidate count; per-recipient cost scales with the whole network |

## Verdict

**zkCoins is meaningfully better-positioned than its closest prior art on the
trust-base axis** — no own chain/token/consensus, no trusted setup, no custodial
operator, a global (not fragmented) anonymity set, and constant-size proofs that hide
history from the recipient (which RGB/Taproot Assets do not, and which Zcash's transparent
dilution and Tornado's pool fragmentation undercut). **But it does not escape the hardest
problem of the Shielded CSV / CSV family — bearer data availability and seed-alone
unrecoverability — and it actually sharpens it**, because moving nullifiers and the
aggregate proof off-chain lets a single missing `BatchBundle` gate the global accumulator
for everyone, and because its safety still rides on an as-yet-unverified recursive circuit
of exactly the kind that produced Zcash's 2026 Orchard counterfeiting bug. Net: a better
*design* than the prior art on decentralization and privacy-completeness; the **same or
harder** operational/liveness problem and a **larger** implementation-soundness surface.
