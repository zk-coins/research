# D2 — Model-vs-Reality Gap

Concept-viability review, dimension D2. What did the TLA+/Apalache verification
abstract away that matters in the real world, and what breaks when reality
diverges from the model?

This is an adversarial reading. The verification is genuinely good work: the
modules state their abstractions openly, the certificates carry honest scope
caveats, and the negative controls show the load-bearing predicates earn their
keep. None of that is in dispute here. The question D2 asks is narrower and
harsher: **a machine-checked safety proof over an abstracted model is only as
strong as the distance between the model and the deployed system.** This
document measures that distance and asks, at each gap, whether the design has an
answer or merely the model does.

## Methodology

- **Baseline.** Spec `zk-coins/docs@b6972b8` (post-`docs#40`), the TLA+ modules
  on branch `formal/apalache-verification` (`module/{Onchain,Proofs,Transport,
  Access,Architecture,Foundations,Assumptions,Adversary}.tla`), the per-property
  `notes.md` and `CERTIFICATE.md`, and `notes/threat-model.md`.
- **Per aspect** I record: (a) **In-model?** — exactly which action/invariant
  covers it, or its documented absence; (b) **What breaks** — the concrete
  failure conceptually reachable when reality diverges; (c) **Modelling-gap vs
  concept-gap** — a *modelling gap* is fixable by extending the TLA+ (the design
  already answers it, the model just simplified); a *concept gap* is one the
  design itself has no answer to, so no amount of further modelling closes it;
  (d) **Severity / Likelihood**; (e) **Mitigation**.
- **Severity scale.** Critical = direct loss of user funds or a permanent
  unrecoverable state; High = stuck funds / safety-relevant divergence requiring
  out-of-band recovery; Medium = degraded UX / liveness / griefing; Low =
  cosmetic or strongly-bounded.
- **Honesty mandate.** No soft-pedaling. Where the design has no answer I say so
  and grade it a concept gap.

A structural observation that frames everything below: **every state-machine
module advances time, confirmations, reorgs, replication, and delivery as
*nondeterministic atomic actions with no clock and no scheduler*.** `Confirm`
promotes an arbitrary subset of pending; `Reorg` drops an arbitrary subset;
`Replicate` adds a holder; `acNow` ticks by 1 with no relation to block height;
`Retry` refreshes a nonce with no backoff timer. This is sound *for safety
invariants* (the model admits strictly more behaviours than reality, so a safety
property proved over the superset holds for the subset — the P02 notes argue
exactly this). But it means **the model is structurally blind to every failure
that is about *timing, ordering, contention, and partial progress* rather than
about reachable end-states.** Most of D2's findings live precisely in that blind
spot, and most of them are liveness/UX/economic, which the certificates already
concede are out of scope (P09 liveness MEDIUM; Apalache 0.58.0 has no fairness).
The D2 contribution is to show how large and load-bearing that conceded region
actually is for a payment system whose entire value proposition is "it works."

## Reality-aspect analysis

### R-D2-1 — Bitcoin reorgs as they really are (the 6-conf cliff)

**In-model?** Partially, and the abstraction is generous in the protocol's
favour. `Onchain.Reorg` reverts an *arbitrary subset* `D ⊆ onPending` and
filters the admitted chain by `Survives(b) == b.batchNullifiers ∩ D = {}`.
`onCompleted` (depth ≥ 6) is **never** touched, gated by
`ReorgWithinBound(depth) == depth < FinalityDepthK = 6` (`Assumptions.tla`,
axiom A12). `Architecture.ArReorg` mirrors this: a `completed` true anchor is
never demoted. So the model's reorgs are (i) bounded strictly below 6 by axiom,
and (ii) over-approximated to arbitrary-subset removal rather than real
suffix/block-granular removal.

Two distinct realities diverge from this:

1. **Real reorgs are suffix reorgs at block granularity, fee-driven.** A reorg
   orphans a *contiguous suffix* of blocks; it does not surgically remove one
   middle batch and keep a later one. The P02 notes already flag this honestly:
   `SelectSeq` over an arbitrary `D` over-approximates to *any-position* removal,
   which **breaks `OnPrevRootContinuity`** (a surviving later batch whose
   `prev_root` commits to a now-removed predecessor). The notes correctly argue
   this does not weaken *no-double-spend* (which reads only the live set `Acc`),
   and certify continuity only over the admit/confirm fragment. **But the gap is
   larger than "a bookkeeping invariant we scoped out":** continuity *is* the
   property that makes deterministic reorg-replay (spec §3.7) well-defined. The
   model proves the *safety* of the accumulator under reorg but does **not**
   verify that the §3.7 canonical replay actually reconstructs the same
   accumulator a different honest node reconstructs — see R-D2-8.

2. **The hard 6-conf assumption vs the real >6 reorg tail.** This is the sharp
   one. Spec §3.9/§3.10/§6.6 and axiom A12 make 6 confirmations a *hard protocol
   constant* and declare any reorg ≥ 6 blocks a **"protocol-failure event …
   outside the spec's guaranteed state machine."** The threat model (M4) says
   the same: "Bitcoin itself is broken at this point … None at the zkCoins
   layer." The model encodes this by *construction* — `depth < 6` is a
   precondition of every `Reorg` action, so a 6-deep reorg is **literally not a
   transition the model can take.** The proof is therefore vacuously silent on
   the one event that actually loses confirmed funds.

**What ACTUALLY happens to user funds in a ≥6 reorg — walked through.** This is
not "Bitcoin is broken so nothing matters." Deep reorgs of 6+ blocks have
occurred on Bitcoin-family chains *without* a hashpower takeover of Bitcoin
itself (historically on lower-hashrate forks; on Bitcoin mainnet the empirical
deep-reorg rate is very low but not zero, and the spec's own number is "no
reorgs deeper than 5"). Suppose one happens:

- A coin's anchoring `BatchInscription` was `completed` (≥6 conf) and a receiver
  Bob *credited* it (§2.3.3 — `completed` is the only creditable state). Alice's
  change coin and her new account-state proof are likewise anchored.
- The reorg orphans the inclusion block. In the new canonical chain the batch is
  gone. The §3.7 rule says reverted batches are "undone and re-applied if the
  bundle is still available and `prev_root` still matches." But a different
  publisher may have won the race on the new tip, so Alice's batch is now
  **stale** and must be re-batched. Meanwhile **Bob already treated the coin as
  final and may have spent it onward** (his own onward spend is now anchored to a
  prev-state that no longer exists, or itself orphaned).
- Result: the nullifier that protected against double-spend is no longer in the
  canonical accumulator. The coin Bob received can, in the new chain, be spent
  again by Alice to a different recipient. **Bob suffers an irreversible loss of
  a credited, finalised coin**, and the protocol's own classification (`completed`
  = creditable) is what told Bob it was safe.

The spec's posture is "this is Bitcoin's tail risk, mitigate at the Bitcoin
layer / wait for more confirmations on extreme value (§6.6)." That is a
defensible *policy* but it is **not a protocol answer** — and the model's
encoding makes the risk *invisible* rather than *bounded*. A reader of the
certificate could reasonably believe "no double-spend, verified unbounded"
covers this; it does not. The honest statement is: **no-double-spend is verified
*conditional on A12*, and A12 is exactly the assumption that fails in the only
scenario where confirmed funds are lost.**

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** The continuity/suffix-reorg piece (1) is a
**modelling gap** — fixable by a suffix-only `Reorg` and a replay-determinism
invariant. The ≥6 reorg piece (2) is a **concept gap** *partially mitigated by
policy*: the design's only lever is "wait longer," which trades the system's
headline latency for tail safety and never reaches zero. There is no
slashing/insurance/checkpoint mechanism at the zkCoins layer to make a deep
reorg recoverable. (Contrast: the threat model's *force-exit* design F5
explicitly measures challenge periods in confirmations and uses a 144-block
window — but that is the separate L2/SSS design, not the base shielded-CSV
protocol modelled here.)

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Critical (irreversible loss of credited
funds). Likelihood Low on Bitcoin mainnet but **non-zero and outside protocol
control**; rises sharply for any lower-hashrate deployment or merged-mined
chain. Net: a Critical-severity, Low-likelihood tail the verification *hides by
construction*.

**Mitigation.** (i) State the A12 dependency *in the no-double-spend headline
claim*, not only in an axiom file. (ii) Make the receive-side confirmation depth
a *deployment policy parameter* with explicit high-value guidance (§6.6 already
gestures at this; promote it to normative per-asset policy). (iii) Consider an
optional checkpoint/finality-gadget or fraud-window construction for high-value
flows — acknowledging it is out of base scope.

### R-D2-2 — Network partition / relay unreachability during the ACK window

**In-model?** The custody-safety *end-state* is modelled and verified; the
partition *dynamics* are abstracted away. `Transport.Drop` is guarded by
`trAckOk = TRUE ∧ Cardinality(trReplicas) ≥ K`, and `TrSenderRetainsUntilSafe`
proves `(~trSenderHasCopy) ⇒ (trAckOk ∧ |trReplicas| ≥ K)` — the sender never
releases the only copy before both a fresh ACK and k-replication. `Retry`
refreshes the nonce; `Ack` admits an adversary-injected ACK and accepts only on
`AckAccepts` (signature + nonce freshness, F17). This is sound and the negative
control (delete `trAckOk` from `Drop` → invariant fails) is real.

**What breaks.** The model has no notion of a partition *healing* and producing
**double delivery**. Walk it through:

- Sender Alice publishes a delivery event; a partition severs Alice from the
  recipient's relay set after the recipient's node *received and verified* the
  bundle (so per §4.5 the recipient credits) but *before* the ACK reaches Alice.
- Alice never sees an ACK, so by §4.2 she retries on backoff "to every relay in
  the recipient's set." When the partition heals, the recipient receives the
  *same* bundle again (a new attempt, fresh nonce).
- **Is the recipient credited once or twice?** Conceptually once: the coin's
  nullifier / the coin id is idempotent, and the receiver's §2.3.3 gate credits
  a *coin*, not a *delivery*. But the model does **not** assert receiver-side
  idempotency anywhere — `Transport.tla` has no recipient-credit variable at
  all; the recipient is just the holder of `op` who signs ACKs. The
  *receiver-credit* logic lives in `P06/property.tla` (`credited` set), which is
  a different state machine that does **not** compose with Transport's retry. So
  "double-delivery on heal is harmless" is **asserted by the prose, not
  verified.** It is *very likely* true (credit is keyed on coin identity, which
  is collision-bound), but it is an unmodelled composition seam.
- The genuinely unbounded-bad case: Alice's retain-until-safe rule means **under
  a long partition Alice holds the only spendable copy indefinitely and cannot
  safely drop it.** That is correct for safety but it means the sender's storage
  obligation is unbounded in partition duration, and there is no modelled
  give-up / timeout. Combined with R-D2-3, a sender that crashes during a long
  partition before persisting the retained copy can lose the coin.

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** Mostly a **modelling gap**: receiver-credit
idempotency under retry should be one invariant over a *composed*
Transport+Receive machine; the design clearly intends idempotency. The
unbounded-retention-under-partition piece is a **mild concept gap** (the design
genuinely has no sender-side give-up that is *safe*; it cannot have one without
risking custody — that is the inherent tension §4.6 names).

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Medium (double-credit is likely benign but
unverified; unbounded retention is a real operational burden). Likelihood Medium
(partitions and relay churn are routine on a Nostr-relay mesh).

**Mitigation.** (i) Compose Transport with the receive gate and prove
credit-idempotency under arbitrary re-delivery. (ii) Specify receiver-side ACK
de-duplication explicitly (the recipient SHOULD re-ACK an already-credited
bundle without re-crediting). (iii) Document the unbounded-retention obligation
as a known operational cost.

### R-D2-3 — Client crash mid-Mint / mid-Send (the persistence race)

**In-model?** **No — not at all.** This is the single largest *category* the
modules do not represent. Every state machine treats actions as atomic and
durable: `Proofs.MintStep`/`SendStep` update `prState`/`prChSet` atomically;
`Transport` tracks `trSenderHasCopy` as a single bit that flips atomically on
`Drop`. There is no crash action, no "witness built but not yet persisted"
intermediate state, no distinction between *in-memory* and *durable* state, and
no ordering relation between "publisher includes the batch on Bitcoin" and
"sender persists its own change/self-delivery."

**What breaks — walked through.** The dangerous interleaving the spec's prose
implies but the model never exercises:

1. Wallet builds the witness for a Send, signs the `SpendRecord` (`message =
   inr ‖ ocr`), and submits it to a publisher.
2. The publisher aggregates it into a `BatchInscription` and **broadcasts it to
   Bitcoin**. The nullifier for Alice's spent coin is now (once the bundle
   verifies) in the global accumulator — **the input coin is consumed,
   irreversibly, from the network's view.**
3. **Alice's wallet crashes before it persists (a) the new `AccountState` proof
   and (b) the self-delivered change bundle.** Per §4.2 self-delivery is what
   makes the change coin and the next account state *recoverable*; per §4.5 step
   5 recovery rebuilds `balances`/`current_pubkey`/`send_counter` from exactly
   those self-delivered bundles.

What is recoverable on restart depends entirely on **where in the
self-delivery+replication sequence the crash landed** — which the model does not
represent:

- If self-delivery completed and reached `k` replicas before the crash, recovery
  (§4.5) finds the change bundle and rebuilds. Safe.
- If the publisher included the batch but Alice crashed **before** self-delivery
  reached *any* independent replica (only her own now-wiped node held it), then
  the input coin is spent on-chain, the change coin's `CoinProof` bundle exists
  **nowhere recoverable**, and §4.5 step 5 "rebuilds from exactly these
  self-addressed change bundles" finds nothing. **Alice's change is
  unspendable** — a real loss proportional to the change amount. The on-chain
  root chain still verifies (R-D2-1/§4.5 step 2), and she can *recognise* her own
  member SpendRecord in the `BatchBundle` (it carries her rotating `Pk_i`), but
  the §4.5 prose is explicit: the change coin's **value** "exist[s] only in the
  `CoinProof` bundles and cannot be derived from the seed or a hash." So she can
  prove *that* she made a transition but cannot reconstruct *what change she is
  owed* if that specific `CoinProof` bundle is gone.

The race that makes this acute: **self-delivery is AFTER witness/sign/submit but
its completion is not ordered against publisher inclusion.** A publisher has
every incentive to inscribe as soon as it has `k`-replicated the *BatchBundle*
(its own DA obligation, §4.6) — which is independent of whether *Alice's
CoinProof self-delivery* reached `k`. So the window "input consumed on-chain,
change not yet durably self-delivered" is **real and publisher-controlled, not
Alice-controlled.**

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** This is a **concept gap with a partial design
answer.** The design's answer is "self-deliver and replicate to `k=3` before
considering the send done, and keep the seed." But the design does **not**
sequence self-delivery-completion *before* publisher inclusion, and it cannot
fully, because the publisher controls inclusion timing. The residual — crash in
the window — has **no protocol recovery**; the only mitigation is wallet
engineering (write-ahead the change `CoinProof` to durable local storage *before*
submitting the SpendRecord, so a crash leaves a re-deliverable copy). That is an
*implementation* obligation the spec gestures at via self-delivery but never
states as a crash-safety / write-ordering requirement, and the model cannot see
it because it has no durability layer.

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity High (loss of change value, no protocol
recovery). Likelihood Medium (client crashes are routine; the vulnerable window
is short but exists on every send and is publisher-timed).

**Mitigation.** (i) Make crash-safe write-ordering normative: persist the
outgoing+change `CoinProof` bundles to durable local storage and begin
self-delivery *before* releasing the `SpendRecord` to a publisher; treat the
local durable copy as replica #1. (ii) Model a `Crash` action and a
durable/volatile state split in a future phase to verify recovery completeness
*across* a crash, not just after total loss with the seed (P09 today assumes the
self-delivered bundles already exist and are replicated — exactly the assumption
this race violates).

### R-D2-4 — Publisher/operator delay and the stale-bundle retry window (UX of limbo)

**In-model?** The *admission outcome* is modelled; the *waiting* is not. A stale
batch (its `prev_root` no longer commits to live `Acc`) simply fails the `[CONT]`
conjunct of `Onchain.Admit` and does not fire — "the §3.6 step-4 stale rejection,
no own action" (module comment). There is no `pending`-due-to-DA variable, no
retry-window clock, no resubmission action. The spec §3.4 supplies the policy:
a stale publisher MUST within ~6 blocks either re-batch onto the live tip or
release the SpendRecords; a spender whose record sat ≥ the window MAY resubmit
elsewhere, and this is "idempotent — admitting the same `nf` twice is impossible
by §3.7."

**Verify the idempotency claim post-#40 (nullifiers off-chain).** The spec's
idempotency argument is: even if the same `SpendRecord` is admitted via two
different publishers, the accumulator transition inserts the `nf` once; a second
insertion is blocked by the first-spend-wins `[GATE]`
(`batchNullifiers ∩ prevRoot = {}`). The model **does** support this: `Admit`
writes `onDoubled` from `batch ∩ Acc` and the `[GATE]` makes the second
admission of the same `nf` a no-op-or-reject; `OnNoDoubleSpend` is verified
unbounded. **So "no double-spend from double-submission" holds.** Good.

But "idempotent ⇒ the spender's risk is only duplicate *effort*" hides two real
costs the model cannot see because it has no clock or fee:

1. **Both publishers may inscribe.** Post-#40, nullifiers are *off-chain* (in the
   BatchBundle); the *on-chain* `BatchInscription` is a 231-byte object a
   publisher pays Bitcoin fees to inscribe. If a spender resubmits and *both*
   the original (no longer stale) and the alternate publisher inscribe batches
   carrying her `nf`, the **second batch fails admission** (its `prev_root`
   won't match once the first is admitted, or `[GATE]` rejects the `nf`) — but
   the second publisher *already paid the Bitcoin fee* for an inscription that
   gets classified `failed`/`stale`. Idempotency is preserved for *safety* but
   **the wasted on-chain fee is a real economic loss borne by a publisher**, who
   will price it back to spenders or refuse marginal resubmissions. The model's
   "duplicate effort" framing undersells this: it is duplicate *Bitcoin fees*,
   the system's scarcest cost.

2. **What the spender SEES while waiting = limbo, unmodelled.** During the retry
   window the spender's coin is in a state that is neither `completed` (can't
   spend onward, can't consider it received) nor `failed` (can't safely
   resubmit yet — doing so early risks the wasted-fee path above). The spec
   gives a *recommended* 6-block (~1h) window but the wallet has **no on-chain
   signal** that distinguishes "my publisher is about to inscribe" from "my
   publisher dropped my record." The §3.10 `pending`-due-to-DA vs
   `pending`-due-to-confirmations distinction (R-D2-8) requires querying a node's
   *verification state*, which a self-sovereign light client may not have. So the
   user experience is: **submit, then wait an opaque ~1 hour during which the
   funds are in limbo and the only recourse (resubmit) may cost money and is
   advised only after the window.** For a payment system this is a serious
   product-level liveness/UX gap, entirely invisible to a model with no time.

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** Safety idempotency: fully covered (no gap).
The wasted-fee and limbo-UX are **concept gaps at the product level** — the
design has *a* policy (the window) but no *mechanism* for the spender to observe
progress or for the wasted-fee cost to be assigned. They are not closable by more
TLA+ (the model is the wrong tool); they need an economic/UX design answer.

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Medium (limbo UX; publisher fee-grief).
Likelihood High (every send passes through this window; publisher contention is
expected — see R-D2-5).

**Mitigation.** (i) Define a spender-observable progress signal (e.g. publisher
returns a signed "accepted into pending batch, target block H" receipt the
wallet can show, distinct from on-chain `completed`). (ii) Specify who eats the
wasted-fee on resubmission (publisher SLA, or spender-funded fee-bump). (iii)
Make the retry window a function of confirmations, not wall-clock.

### R-D2-5 — Multi-publisher race on the same prev_root (the serialization bottleneck)

**In-model?** The *race resolution* is modelled and is in fact elegant; the
*contention dynamics and scaling consequence* are entirely absent. In
`Onchain.tla` the accumulator advances *strictly sequentially*: `Admit` requires
`RootCommitsSet(cand.prevRoot, Acc)` — `prev_root` must commit to the **current
live accumulator**. Two publishers racing on the same `prev_root` both build
valid batches, but only one can win the Bitcoin canonical ordering (height, then
reveal-tx index, §3.6 step 4); the other's `prev_root` no longer matches `Acc`
and it fails `[CONT]`. The model captures the *winner-take-one* correctly. There
is **no** action representing *wasted proving work*, *latency under contention*,
or *throughput*. There is no notion that `Admit` is a global serialization point.

**What breaks — the scaling consequence, stated bluntly.** The global nullifier
accumulator advances **one batch at a time, globally, ordered by Bitcoin block
position.** This is the design's correctness backbone (it is *what makes*
first-spend-wins a total order) but it is also a **hard global serialization
bottleneck**:

- Every publisher who wants to advance the accumulator must build an
  `AggregateBatchProof` against the *current* `prev_root`. The moment any
  publisher's batch is admitted, **every other in-flight proof built against the
  old root is stale** and must be re-built against the new root. Under
  contention with `P` active publishers, in the worst case `P−1` of every `P`
  proofs are wasted, and proving an aggregate recursive Plonky2 proof is
  *expensive* (seconds to tens of seconds — cf. the project's own R2 baseline
  notes on circuit-build cost). This is **latency multiplication under
  contention**: effective system throughput is bounded by *one* successful batch
  per the time it takes to (re)prove-and-inscribe, *regardless of how many
  publishers exist*. More publishers do not increase throughput; past a point
  they *decrease* it via wasted re-proving.

- **Adversarial stale-forcing.** An adversary running a publisher can *cheaply*
  keep the accumulator-advance contended: inscribe small batches frequently to
  keep bumping `prev_root`, forcing honest publishers to continually re-prove.
  The adversary pays Bitcoin fees but imposes *proving* cost on everyone else —
  an asymmetric griefing vector (cheap to mount, expensive to suffer). The
  model's `[CONT]` gate makes this *safe* (no double-spend) but the model has no
  way to express that it is *slow* or *grief-able*. The threat model does not
  enumerate this either (its publisher/race entries are about the separate SSS
  design).

This is, in my reading, **the most underappreciated structural risk in the
design**, because it is invisible to a safety-only model yet it directly caps the
system's headline capability (payments per unit time) and hands an adversary a
cheap latency weapon. The single-sequential-accumulator is to zkCoins what the
global state lock is to a database: correct, and the throughput ceiling.

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** **Concept gap.** The strict-sequential
accumulator is intrinsic to the post-#40 design (it is *why* first-spend-wins is
a clean total order). You cannot make it concurrent without giving up the very
property the verification certifies. The mitigations are all *amortization*, not
removal: bigger batches (one inscription commits many spends → fewer
serialization points per payment), publisher coordination/leasing of the
prev_root, and fee-market prioritization. The model cannot help here; this needs
a throughput/economics design (see D7 scaling review) and an explicit
contention/grief analysis.

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity High (caps throughput; cheap griefing).
Likelihood High at any non-trivial adoption — contention is the *expected* steady
state of a successful system, not an edge case.

**Mitigation.** (i) Maximize batch size so payments-per-inscription is high and
serialization points per payment is low. (ii) Specify a publisher coordination
mechanism (prev_root lease / round-robin / fee-priority) to cut wasted re-proving.
(iii) Add an explicit adversarial-contention section to the threat model. (iv)
Quantify the realistic ceiling (batches/hour × spends/batch) in the scaling doc.

### R-D2-6 — Time-skew: block height vs wall-clock; abstract clocks; backoff timers

**In-model?** Time is modelled only as an *abstract monotone counter with no
units and no relation to anything else*. `Access.acNow` is an `Int` that `Tick`
increments by 1; challenge `expiry` and grant windows are `≥ acNow` comparisons.
`Onchain.blockAnchorHeight` is an `Int` carried as `anchorOk : Bool` (the §3.5
strict-ancestor + gap≤100 bound is *not* simulated, just asserted as a precond).
`Transport` backoff (30s…1h) is "abstracted to a non-deterministic Retry action…
timing is liveness, irrelevant to the safety claims" (module comment). So there
are **three independent notions of time** in the real system — Bitcoin block
height, wall-clock for challenge/grant expiry and ACK backoff, and confirmation
depth — and the model represents each as a *separate, unrelated abstract scalar*
with no skew between them.

**What breaks.** In reality these clocks *drift against each other*:

- **block_anchor.height vs wall-clock issuance window.** §3.5 evaluates the §6.5
  issuance validity-window height check **in-circuit against
  `block_anchor.height`** (prover-supplied, the tip the proof was built against),
  *not* against the actual later inclusion height. The gap is bounded to ≤100
  blocks (~16h). The model carries this as a single `anchorOk` bit, so it cannot
  see that **an issuance valid at proving time can be included up to ~16h later**,
  during which the wall-clock-defined validity intent and the
  block-height-defined check can disagree (e.g. a "valid until date D" intent vs
  a height that maps to a different wall-clock time under variable block
  intervals). This is a *minor* correctness-vs-intent skew but it is real and
  unmodelled.

- **Challenge/grant expiry (`acNow`) vs the host's real clock.** The Access
  endpoint expires challenges and view-grants against a node-local clock. Two
  honest nodes serving the same subject can have skewed clocks, so a grant near
  its `expiry` is accepted by one node and rejected by another — *inconsistent
  view-release across nodes*. Safety is preserved (no over-release) but it is a
  liveness/consistency wart the abstract single `acNow` hides (each module has
  its own clock; there is no shared time).

- **ACK backoff vs partition duration (ties to R-D2-2).** The 30s→1h backoff is
  pure liveness and unmodelled. Real consequence: under intermittent
  connectivity the exponential cap at 1h means a recipient that comes back online
  may wait up to an hour for the next retry — delivery latency the model treats
  as instantaneous.

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** Mostly **modelling gap** (clocks could be
unified and skew injected in a future model), with one **mild concept gap**: the
issuance-window-against-anchor-height design *chooses* to bind to height not
wall-clock, which is a deliberate, defensible trade (height is provable
in-circuit) but means "validity window" never means exactly what a wall-clock
user expects. That is a design decision, not a bug, but it should be documented
as a semantic, not hidden behind `anchorOk`.

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Low–Medium (consistency warts, no fund
loss). Likelihood Medium (clock skew between independent operators is the norm).

**Mitigation.** (i) Document that all expiry/window semantics are *block-height*
or *node-local-clock*, never global wall-clock. (ii) For view-grants near expiry,
recommend a skew margin. (iii) Recovery/receive should always re-anchor to a
stated tip (the spec already requires this for NAV).

### R-D2-7 — Adversarial timing: deadline-edge withholding, reorg-timed republication, ACK timing-grief

**In-model?** **No.** Every adversary capability in `Adversary.tla` is a
*non-temporal* predicate: `AdvMayControlRelays(honestReplicas) == honestReplicas
≥ 1`, `AdvMayPublish`, `AdvMayControlNode` (drop/delay/reorder/lie "freely"). The
word "delay" appears in the prose of `AdvMayControlNode` but there is **no action
parameterized by *when***. F17 (ack nonce) defeats *replay* of a captured ACK
across attempts — `AckAccepts` checks `ackNonce = trAttemptNonce`. But F17 is
about *value freshness*, not *timing*. Nothing in the model represents an
adversary that acts *at a chosen moment relative to a deadline*.

**What breaks.** Three timing-precise griefing vectors the model is blind to:

1. **Deadline-edge BatchBundle withholding.** §3.6 step 5 leaves an inscription
   `pending` if no replica is reached "within the node's bundle-fetch deadline."
   A publisher that controls (or colludes with) the bundle's replicas can
   **withhold the BatchBundle until just before the fetch deadline at scanning
   nodes**, maximizing the time the inscription sits in `pending`-due-to-DA and
   different nodes disagree on whether it admitted (R-D2-8). It is *not* a
   double-spend (safety holds) but it is a **targeted liveness/consistency
   attack** that the §4.6 "k-independent replicas + scanners retain forever"
   discipline mitigates only *after* at least one honest scanner has fetched —
   and the attacker times the withholding to stay under that. Cost to attacker:
   ~zero (just don't serve). Cost to victims: a window of inconsistent
   accumulator views.

2. **Reorg-timed republication.** Combine R-D2-1 with timing: an adversary that
   can effect even a *bounded* (<6) reorg can time the orphaning of a batch and
   the republication of a *competing* batch on the new tip to win the new
   canonical ordering (§3.6 step 4 secondary key = reveal-tx index). The model's
   `Reorg` drops an arbitrary subset and `Admit` re-races, so the *outcome* set
   is covered — but the model cannot express that the *attacker chooses the
   timing to flip which of two racing batches wins* (the spec's §3.10 "a reorg
   MAY change which of two inscriptions racing on the same prev_root is rejected"
   — exactly this, acknowledged in prose, unmodelled as an adversarial *choice*).
   For a value-receiving Bob at <6 conf this is a real (if bounded) front-running
   surface.

3. **ACK timing-grief / bundle-drop griefing.** F17 stops ACK *replay*; it does
   not stop an adversary-relay from **dropping the ACK every time** to force
   Alice into perpetual retry (keeping her storage pinned, R-D2-2) while still
   letting the *delivery* through so the recipient credits. Net: recipient is
   paid, sender can never safely drop her copy. The model's `Drop` guard makes
   this *safe* but the *griefing* (unbounded sender retention via selective ACK
   suppression) is invisible — `AdvMayControlRelays` needs only `honestReplicas
   ≥ 1`, which is about *availability of the blob*, not *delivery of the ACK*.

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** (1) and (3) are **concept gaps** —
liveness/griefing the design accepts as "availability, not safety" (§4.6) and for
which it has *no* mechanism, only the hope that ≥1 honest replica/relay exists.
(2) is a **modelling gap layered on the R-D2-1 concept gap**: the timing-choice
could be modelled, but the underlying reorg-flips-winner is an acknowledged
bounded risk with the policy answer "wait for `completed`."

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Medium (griefing/liveness, bounded
front-running). Likelihood Medium (cheap to mount for anyone running a relay or
publisher).

**Mitigation.** (i) Add a timing-aware adversary to a future model (deadline
parameter on withholding actions) to *bound* the inconsistency window. (ii) Make
the bundle-fetch deadline and scanner-retain rule normative with a *minimum*
honest-fetch quorum, not just `k≥1`. (iii) Document ACK-suppression griefing and
give the sender a safe bounded give-up tied to *confirmed k-replication of the
blob itself* (if the blob is provably k-replicated, the sender's drop is safe
even without an ACK — decouple custody-safe-drop from ACK-receipt).

### R-D2-8 — pending-due-to-DA: two honest nodes classify the same record differently

**In-model?** The *safety* consequence is modelled (an un-admitted batch
contributes nothing); the *cross-node consistency* consequence is **explicitly
abstracted away by axiom A13.** `Onchain.tla`: "a batch sitting un-admitted is
exactly onPending-due-to-DA … the accumulator-bearing invariants are insensitive
to the reason an un-admitted batch is un-admitted, so the sub-state needs no own
var." `Architecture.tla` and P06 both *assume* A13: "every honest node shares
the one true chain view," "the receiver reads the on-chain machine's single true
view." The P06 notes state it plainly: A13 eclipse-resistance is **an assumption,
not a theorem** — out of frame.

**What breaks — the real-world consistency story, stated honestly.** The spec
§3.7 (Path-B light client) and §3.10 promise that two honest nodes at the *same
tip* derive the *same* `(completed, pending-DA, pending-confs)` classification.
But §3.6 step 5 and §4.6 make this **conditional on bundle availability**, and
the spec itself admits the failure mode in §4.6: universal unavailability of a
BatchBundle "would leave its accumulator transition forever unconfirmable — a
**soft fork of the network** into 'nodes that admitted it before DA loss' and
'nodes that never could.'" That is the exact divergence:

- Node X fetched the BatchBundle, verified the AggregateBatchProof, **admitted**
  the batch → its `Acc` includes those nullifiers; it classifies the batch
  `pending`/`completed` and will *reject* a later spend of those coins as a
  double-spend.
- Node Y *cannot reach any replica* → the batch is `pending`-due-to-DA; its `Acc`
  does **not** include those nullifiers; it will *accept* a spend that X rejects.

So **at the same Bitcoin tip, two honest nodes hold different accumulators and
give opposite double-spend answers** until DA heals. The model is blind to this
because it carries *one* global `onPending/onCompleted` (A13 collapses all honest
nodes to one view). The P06 "identical classification" guarantee is therefore
**true only under the unmodelled A13 *and* full bundle availability** — two
assumptions, the second of which §4.6 itself says can fail.

The practical danger is not a *protocol* double-spend (each individual node is
internally consistent and first-spend-wins holds *within* a node's view). It is
that **a self-sovereign receiver Bob, querying node Y, can be made to credit a
coin that node X already knows is double-spent** — because Y hasn't seen the
batch that spent it. §6.3 multi-node fan-out partially defends this (query
several nodes, keep the verifying answer), but fan-out resolves *forks* (same
counter, different ash) and *sync lag* (different counters); it does **not**
resolve *DA-divergence* (same tip, different `Acc` because Y lacks a bundle X
has) — and the §3.7 Path-B rule "MUST NOT accept a non-membership answer against
an inscribed-but-not-bundle-verified root" is precisely the patch, but it pushes
the burden onto the *light client* to detect that its serving node is behind on
DA, which a light client structurally *cannot* verify on its own (it has no
bundle to check).

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** **Concept gap.** Honest-node accumulator
divergence under partial DA is *intrinsic* to "on-chain commits only a root;
membership lives off-chain in bundles." The design's answers are all
*availability mitigations* (k=3, scanners-retain-forever, Path-B
must-query-bundle-verified-root) — they make divergence *rare and self-healing*
but they cannot make it *impossible*, because that would require putting the
nullifier set on-chain (which #40 explicitly removed for privacy/cost). This is
the deepest model-vs-reality gap: **the verified property "identical
classification at the same tip" is exactly the property reality does not
guarantee.** The certificate's P06 HIGH is correct *given A13+DA*; D2's point is
that A13+DA is the whole ballgame and it is assumed, not proved or even modelled.

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity High (a receiver can credit a network-known
double-spend during a DA gap; bounded by DA healing). Likelihood Medium (depends
on `k`, operator independence, and adversarial withholding R-D2-7; rare in the
healthy case, deliberately inducible by an attacker).

**Mitigation.** (i) Make Path-B's "query latest *bundle-verified* root" normative
and give light clients a way to *detect* a DA-behind node (e.g. nodes attest
their DA-completeness up to a stated tip with a signed marker the client can
cross-check across the fan-out). (ii) Receivers of meaningful value SHOULD
fan-out *and* require agreement on the bundle-verified root, not just on
`completed`. (iii) Model honest-node divergence by replacing the single global
`Acc` with per-node accumulators parameterized by each node's fetched-bundle set;
prove the *bounded-divergence + eventual-agreement* property explicitly rather
than assuming A13.

### R-D2-9 — Fee spikes, mempool eviction, and publisher UTXO/dust management (added)

**In-model?** **No.** `Onchain.tla` has no fee, no mempool, no UTXO. Inscriptions
appear in `onAdmittedChain` atomically; there is no representation of a reveal
transaction sitting unconfirmed, being fee-bumped, or being evicted.

**What breaks.** The single on-chain object is a 231-byte `BatchInscription`
reveal transaction. Real Bitcoin consequences:

- **Fee spikes make inscriptions expensive and slow.** At high mempool pressure
  a 231-byte inscription (plus the commit tx of the reveal/commit inscription
  pattern) can cost materially, and *unconfirmed for a long time*. The
  block_anchor gap≤100 (~16h) bounds how stale the anchor may be at inclusion; if
  fees spike such that the reveal cannot confirm within 100 blocks of its anchor,
  the inscription is **rejected as stale-anchored** (§3.5 condition 2) and the
  whole batch must be re-proved and re-published — wasted proving *and* wasted
  commit-tx fees. So fee spikes don't just raise cost; they can *invalidate
  in-flight batches*, amplifying R-D2-4/R-D2-5.

- **Mempool eviction / RBF.** A reveal tx evicted under fee pressure (or stuck
  below the dynamic mempool min-fee) leaves the batch un-inscribed; spenders sit
  in limbo (R-D2-4) with no on-chain signal. If the publisher RBF-bumps, fine;
  if not, the SpendRecords must be released per §3.4.

- **Publisher UTXO/dust management.** A publisher inscribing continuously needs a
  steady supply of spendable UTXOs to fund commit+reveal transactions and avoid
  dust fragmentation. This is an operational liveness dependency entirely outside
  the model: a publisher that runs out of well-formed funding UTXOs **stalls the
  accumulator** for everyone routed through it (ties to the R-D2-5 serialization
  bottleneck — a single under-funded dominant publisher is a liveness single
  point of failure).

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** **Concept gap at the economic/operational
layer** (the model is the wrong tool; this needs a publisher-economics analysis).
The *protocol* is safe under all of it (a stuck/evicted reveal just doesn't
admit), but the *system's liveness and cost* depend on fee markets and publisher
treasury management the spec barely addresses (§3.8 "fees and economics" is thin).

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Medium (liveness/cost; can cascade into
batch invalidation via the 100-block anchor bound). Likelihood High during fee
spikes, which are a recurring Bitcoin reality.

**Mitigation.** (i) Publisher SLAs covering RBF/fee-bumping and funding-UTXO
management. (ii) Re-examine the 100-block anchor bound against worst-case
confirmation times under fee spikes — it may be too tight, turning fee spikes
into batch-invalidation events. (iii) Add publisher economics to the scaling/D7
analysis.

### R-D2-10 — The "honest = omission-only" abstraction vs real operator misbehaviour (added)

**In-model?** Yes, as a deliberate and load-bearing simplification.
`Architecture.tla`: a dishonest node "CANNOT forge a valid recursive proof …
modelled as pure OMISSION: it either returns a truthful snapshot or withholds.
There is no 'forged but verifying' answer in the reachable space." This is sound
*given* A1 (knowledge soundness) and the wallet's client-side re-verification.

**What breaks — where the abstraction leaks.** "Omission-only" is correct for
*proof-bearing* answers. But operators do more than answer queries:

- An operator runs the **Access pull endpoint** (R-D2-6) and decides *which*
  view-grants to honor, *when* to expire them, and *whether* to serve at all —
  selective service is "omission" in the model but is **discriminatory
  censorship** in reality (the threat model's X3 names this for the SSS design;
  it applies equally to a zkCoins node). Omission-modelling makes censorship look
  like benign withholding.
- An operator is the **DA replica** (R-D2-8) and the **publisher** (R-D2-5);
  "omission" by a *publisher* is the serialization-griefing and DA-withholding of
  R-D2-5/R-D2-7, which are *active* timing strategies, not passive silence.
- The model's `CorruptNode` only flips `honest := FALSE` (→ `verifies = FALSE`).
  It cannot represent an operator that is *honest on proofs but adversarial on
  timing/availability/service* — which is the *realistic* operator threat (a
  rational operator maximizing fees/grief, not a proof-forger).

**Modelling-gap vs concept-gap.** **Modelling gap** in principle (a richer
operator action set could be added) but it **inherits the concept gaps** of
R-D2-5/7/8: even modelled, the underlying liveness/censorship has only
availability-mitigations, no protocol cure. The honesty point for the
certificate: "at least one honest node suffices" (`ArOneHonestSuffices`,
Requirement 4) is verified *for state-selection correctness*, but the realistic
operator threat is not state-forgery (which one honest node defeats) — it is
*coordinated timing/availability degradation*, against which "one honest node"
does **not** suffice (you need one honest node *that has the bundle and serves it
in time*, which R-D2-8 shows is not guaranteed).

**Severity / Likelihood.** Severity Medium–High (censorship/liveness at the
operator layer; defeats the "one honest node" intuition for availability).
Likelihood Medium (rational operators, not just byzantine ones).

**Mitigation.** (i) Reframe "one honest node suffices" precisely: it suffices for
*state-selection safety*, **not** for availability/timeliness. (ii) Model a
rational (not just omitting) operator. (iii) Cross-reference D3 (operator-trust
surface) — this is the same surface seen from the timing angle.

## Recommendations

Ordered by leverage.

1. **Promote the A12 dependency into every fund-safety headline (R-D2-1).** The
   no-double-spend claim is `conditional on no ≥6 reorg`; the model makes the one
   fund-losing scenario *unreachable by construction*. State it where the claim
   is read, not only in `Assumptions.tla`. Add per-asset confirmation-depth
   policy for high value. This is documentation, not code, and it is the highest
   integrity-of-claim fix.

2. **Treat the global single-sequential accumulator as the primary scaling and
   griefing risk (R-D2-5), not a footnote.** Quantify the realistic throughput
   ceiling (batches/block × spends/batch), specify publisher coordination to
   stop wasted re-proving, and add an adversarial-contention section. This is the
   risk most hidden by a safety-only model and most likely to bite a *successful*
   deployment.

3. **Make crash-safe write-ordering a normative wallet requirement (R-D2-3).**
   Persist outgoing+change `CoinProof` bundles durably and begin self-delivery
   *before* releasing the `SpendRecord`. Without this, every send has a
   publisher-timed window in which a crash loses the change with no protocol
   recovery. P09's recovery proof *assumes* the self-delivered bundles already
   exist — exactly what this race can violate.

4. **Close the honest-node DA-divergence story (R-D2-8).** Replace the single
   global `Acc` (A13 collapse) with per-node accumulators over each node's
   fetched-bundle set, and prove *bounded-divergence + eventual-agreement*
   instead of assuming identical classification. Give light clients a way to
   detect a DA-behind serving node. This is the deepest model-vs-reality gap:
   the verified "identical classification at the same tip" is the property
   reality does not provide.

5. **Add timing to the adversary and a clock to the model where it is
   load-bearing (R-D2-2, R-D2-6, R-D2-7).** A timing-aware adversary lets the
   inconsistency/limbo windows be *bounded* rather than *invisible*; decouple
   custody-safe-drop from ACK-receipt (provable k-replication of the blob should
   suffice) to kill ACK-suppression griefing.

6. **Add a publisher-economics and fee analysis (R-D2-4, R-D2-9).** Spender-
   observable progress signal, who-pays-the-wasted-fee on resubmission, RBF/
   funding-UTXO SLAs, and a re-check of the 100-block anchor bound against
   worst-case fee-spike confirmation times.

A closing honest note on what the verification *is*: it is a rigorous proof that
*the abstracted state machine never reaches an unsafe end-state under the stated
axioms*. Every D2 finding is either (a) a behaviour the axioms assume away
(A12 deep-reorg, A13 single-view) — where the proof is correct but the
assumption is the actual risk; or (b) a *timing/contention/durability/economic*
property the safety-only, clockless, crashless model is structurally incapable of
expressing. Neither is a flaw in the verification *as a safety proof*. Both mean
the certificate's green checkmarks should be read as "safe **if** A12, A13, full
DA, durable clients, and an uncontended honest publisher set hold" — and D2's
job is to say plainly that those five conditions are where the real risk lives.
